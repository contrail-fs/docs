#### GENERAL

The airplane is equipped with two ice protection systems:

- One system using engine hot air, and providing protection to:
  - The wing leading edge slats.
  - The air intake lips of each engine.
  - Center engine S-duct.
  - The spinner of each engine, if elliptical (F50 087 SB not applied), (refer to Engines chapter).
- An electrical system ensuring heating of:
  - The pitot probes, static ports and angle-of-attack vanes.
  - The cockpit windows.
  - The Pt2 and Tt2 engine sensors (see Engine chapter).

The pilot and copilot windshields are provided with electrically actuated wipers.

The front windshields are demisted by blown air independent of the air conditioning system.

Each cabin window is demisted by an individual desiccant squib system.

A windshield ice detection light is mounted in the cockpit (see LIGHTING chapter, p. 4–70–21). Moreover, two wing ice detection lights may be mounted as optional equipment (option 33–40–01 or F50 009 SB), see LIGHTING chapter, P. 4–70–25.

#### 1 − ANTI−ICING BY HOT AIR

#### A − General

The anti−icing systems use engine bleed air as follows.

- − High pressure air for the side nacelle and center engine S duct air intakes.
- − Mixture of HP and LP air for the inside of the center engine S duct and the wing leading edges.
- − Engine own HP bleed air for the elliptical spinner (F50 0087 SB not applied). ENG anti−icing controls and indications are provided whether conical or elliptical spinners are mounted. However, in the case of a non anti−iced conical spinner, a simulation box is installed.

Both types of spinners may equip the engines of a same airplane.

#### B − Side engine anti−icing

The engines and nacelles are anti−iced by engine HP air.

#### − Nacelle air intake anti−icing:

The HP bleed air flowing through the hinged duct at 12 o'clock is ducted to the anti−icing valve through a line fitted with a restrictor and two expansion sleeves.

Downstream of the valve, this line enters the air intake box and is connected to a circular manifold that distributes hot air throughout the box.

This air circulates through the box aftward between the air intake internal wall and a shroud and is evacuated outside through side louvers.

There is an indicating pressure switch downstream of the valve, adjusted to 4 psi 0.28 relative bar in increasing pressure.

− S duct air intake anti−icing:

Similar to the side nacelle air−intakes.

− S duct anti−icing:

The upper concave portion of the S duct needs ice protection.

#### D - Wing leading edge anti-icing

The wing leading edge inboard and outboard slats are anti-iced (in extended or retracted position) by hot air coming from the common bleed air distribution line.

- When ISOLATION valve is closed, the air comes from engine 1 and 3 bleed ports:
  - 1° Main engine 1 and 3 LP ports.
  - 2° HP1 and HP3 ports; the two solenoid valves will open if AIRFRAME switch is ON.
- When ISOLATION valve is open, the air is a mixture of bleed air from engines 1 and 3 with engine 2 main bleed air.

#### − Air system description:

From the common line, air is routed as follows:

- 1° Through the anti−icing valves in parallel.
- 2° The hot air is ducted to the wing, under the fuselage along A/C centerline. The duct is divided into two branches at frame 32 (one for each wing).
- 3° Hot air is then distributed to the leading edge.

#### Wing root:

The wing root fixed leading edge which is uncovered when the inboard slat is extended is anti−iced by hot air ducted into the space between the fixed leading edge skin and the internal shroud. Air is then evacuated aftwards.

Slat anti−icing air supply:

The supply line is routed from the wing−to−fuselage fairing to the front of the wing forward spar.

- − A first telescopic tube supplies the inboard slat distribution manifold.
- − A second telescopic tube supplies the outboard slat air distribution manifold.
- − A pressure switch between the two above tubes is used for indication (it is set for to 4 psi in increasing pressure).

#### − Control and indication:

Leading edge anti−icing is controlled by AIRFRAME three−position (NORM, ST−BY, OFF) switch on the overhead panel.

- Setting this switch to NORM will cause:
  - − The normal airframe antl−icing valve to open.
- Setting switch to ST−BY will cause:
  - − The stand−by anti−icing valve to open, and simultaneously the normal valve to close.
- Setting the switch to OFF will cause:
  - − The normal and stand−by valves to close.

When switch is in NORM or ST−BY, HP1 and HP3 valves are open (if HP1 and HP3 switches are in AUTO).

#### E – Hot air anti-icing operating instructions

#### 1) In flight:

The anti-icing system is a preventing means, not designed for eliminating ice. As a consequence, the anti-icing must be turned on in flight as soon as TAT drops below +5 °C, in freezing conditions.

#### 2) Take-off:

If icing conditions prevail on the ground, prior to taking-off, only engine anti-icing is authorized (ENG1, ENG2, ENG3 switches on).

Wing leading edge anti-icing (AIRFRAME switch) will be turned on only after the landing gear has been retracted.

Refer to the Flight Manual for the effects on performance of anti-icing operation.

#### 3) If icing conditions are long to be detected:

Ice may already have accumulated on the air intakes and on the slats.

Turning on anti-icing will cause lumps of ice to melt off and be ingested by the engines.

To limit the risk of engine flame-out:

- Set the three start selectors to AIRSTART.
- Turn on ENG 1 and ENG 3 anti-icing, and
  - ---- 30 seconds later:
- Turn on ENG 2 anti-icing, then
  - ---- 30 seconds later:
- Set AIR FRAME switch to NORM.

#### B − Windshield electrical anti−icing and demisting

#### 1 ) Anti−icing of the three windshield panes:

Pilot, copilot and center front windshields are likely to catch ice and must therefore be anti−iced.

The built−in heating network is located between the two outer layers of the panes.

− Electrical system:

#### General

There are two systems:

- One ensures heating of the pilot windshield and the left halt of the front windshield.
- The other heating of the copilot windshield and the right half of the front windshield.

Each circuit has its own temperature control system. In the event of failure of one system, temperature of that system will be controlled by the other.

#### Pilot and copilot windshield characteristics:

Both are identical. Each heating network covers 17−6 sq dm (1.9 sq ft) and has a resistance of 0.467 ohms. There are two independent temperature probes in each windshield; one is used for temperature control, the other is in stand−by and can be used if the first has failed, to avoid replacing a window for a mere probe failure.

#### Front windshield heating characteristics:

Each half network covers a surface of 8.6 sq dm (0.95 sq ft) for a resistance of 0.212 ohms.

There is no temperature control probe. Temperature is controlled in each half windshield by the pilot or copilot windshield probe to which it is connected.

#### Temperature control:

- − The regulator cuts off supply to the system as soon as the temperature sensed by the probe reaches 30°C (86°F), and switches it on, when the temperature has dropped to 25°C (77°F).
- − If any of the following failure occurs:
  - Probe cut off, or too high resistance (corresponding to a sensed temperature of +60°C (+140°F)).
  - Short in the probe, or too low resistance (corresponding to a sensed temperature of −70°C (−94°F)).

#### In such case:

- XFR amber light illuminates on the overhead panel.
- The other regulator is activated. System performance is not lost although one regulation system has failed.

#### Controls:

PILOT and COPILOT three−position switches control operation of the regulation systems. They are mounted on the overhead panel in the "WINDSHIELD" zone.

OFF: system is off.

NORM: The heating networks of the main windshield and half−front windshield are connected in series.

#### 3 − RAIN AND MIST REPELLENT SYSTEMS

#### A − Windshield wipers

The pilot and copilot front windshields are equipped with electrically−operated wipers. They are stowed in a housing at the bottom of the windshield.

Pilot windshield wiper is powered from primary bus A (28 Vdc).

Copilot wiper is powered from auxiliary bus D (28 Vdc).

#### Controls

The WIPER are activated by means of WIPER switches, to the left and right lower part of the upper panel ; they have three positions: ON − OFF − PARK.

- − When ON, the wipers are commanded to operate.
- − When OFF, the wipers stop where they are.
- − When on PARK, the wipers go to the stowed position.

Park is a momentary position. Switch goes back to OFF when released.

#### B − Interior demisting of the windshield wiper

The inner surface of the three front windshields may be demisted, as desired, by air blown through distribution manifolds housed in the glareshield.

This system comprises:

- − A (35 Watts − 115 VAC − 400Hz) blower located below the instrument panel, forward of the pedestal.
- − A heating resistor of 450 watts.
- − A duct routing the air from the pedestal to the manifolds.

- Safety devices:

- Regulation thermostat adjusted to 60 °C (140 °F).
- A thermal fuse, cutting off power to the resistor in case of overheating (blower failure combined with thermostat failure). It is adjusted to 126 °C (260 °F).
- Speed adjusting device:

It varies the blower rotation speed (thus the blown air flow). It automatically sets into operation the heating resistor when the power to the blower is 90 % of the maximum power.

This system is controlled by DEFOG handle next to NOSE control, on copilot console.

- Interior demisting operation:
  - Handle fully pulled to the rear: the blower runs steadily (1,000 rpm approximately) and ventilates the pedestal instruments.
  - Handle at mid-course: the blower runs faster, but the air is not heated.
  - Handle pushed forward: hot demisting air is supplied.

NOTE: In latter case, the blown air reaches its maximum temperature after three minutes (time for the resistor to heat).

#### C - Passenger cabin window demisting

The space between the two layers of the windows are demisted by desiccant squibs (1 by window), through two ports provided at lower part of the inner layer.

Remark: The windows of the cockpit are also demisted in the same manner.
