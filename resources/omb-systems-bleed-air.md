**BLEED AIR** 

#### 1 - GENERAL

- Each engine has 3 bleed air ports:

SYSTEM DESCRIPTION

- 2 Low Pressure (LP) bleed ports at level of the last stage of the axial compressor. They are at 3 o'clock and 9 o'clock on the engine.
- 1 High Pressure (HP) bleed port downstream of the centrifugal compressor (last stage of the compressor). It is located on top of the engine (12 o'clock).
- All bleed ports supply the airplane pneumatic systems except the RH LP port of the RH engine, which is capped off.
- Each engine has also an additional internal HP bleed which is used for engine anti-icing.
- As a rule, each bleed air system is equipped with check valves which prevent air from returning to the engines when they are shut-off or running at low rpm.
- The APU provides additional LP bleed air for the conditioning and pressurization systems (OPTION).

#### A - MAIN LOW PRESSURE BLEED AIR

  - LH engine right port.

#### B − AUXILIARY LOW PRESSURE BLEED AIR

The auxiliary bleed air ports are the LH engine LH port and center engine LH port.

#### 1 ) Characteristics

Temperatures and pressures are the same as for the main LP bleed air systems. They are independent of the main LP bleed air systems.

#### 2 ) Description

Air from the LH ports of the LH and center engines ensures:

- − Pressurization of the 2 hydraulic reservoirs.
- − Pressurization of the fuel tanks:
  - Wing tanks.
  - Feeder tanks.
- − The LB bleed air system and the fuel pressurization pressure reducing valve drains are connected to an automatic fuselage drain.
- − The right engine RH port is capped off.

#### B − HP BLEED AIR

The section of each bleed air line is such that the maximum bleed air flow is limited in order:

- − To limit the temperature of downstream LP/HP mixed air.
- − To limit the leaks in the event of rupture of the line or leak in the downstream system, so as to minimize the effects of such failure on engine operation.

#### 1 ) Center engine

The main line is divided into three sub−lines:

- − One supplies the common low pressure system through a high pressure solenoïd valve.
- − Another supplies both conditioning systems through a Pressure Regulating Valve (PRV).
- − The auxiliary line supplies the engine and the S duct with anti−icing air through three solenoïd valves.

#### 2 ) Side engines

The line is divided into two sub−systems:

- − One supplies the common low pressure system through a high pressure solenoïd valve.
- − The other supplies the nacelle with anti−icing air through a solenoïd valve.

#### 3 ) LH engine and center engine

A high pressure pick−off supplies the pressurization jet pump.

#### C − ENGINE INTERNAL HP BLEED AIR

Each engine is provided with a bleed air port for its own anti−icing.

When engine is equipped with an elliptical spinner, spinner anti−icing air is supplied from this port, through a valve.

When engine is equipped with a non anti−iced conical spinner, duct from this HP port is capped off.

#### 4 − MAIN BLEED AIR DISTRIBUTION AND CONTROL

#### A − LOW PRESSURE BLEED AIR

The three engine main LP bleed air port and optional APU bleed air port supply a common line which in turn supplies the following systems through conditioning valves:

- − RH conditioning system (cockpit − baggage compartment).
- − LH conditioning system (passenger cabin).

#### Engine anti−icing bleed air

Only the distribution sub−line downstream of the common line has a control valve.

#### APU BLEED light

This amber light is located on the warning panel.

- − If the APU is shut down or running at an rpm lower than 95 %, this light indicates that the bleed air valve is not fully closed.
- − If the APU is running (rpm > 95 %), although one of the throttles is above 54 % N1, it indicates that automatic closing function of the valve is inoperative.

#### B − HIGH PRESSURE BLEED AIR

The main portion of each line meets the corresponding low pressure bleed air lines through a high pressure valve.

The three low pressure and the three high pressure bleed air lines supply.

The common distribution line is therefore supplied with bleed air from the 3 main LP and the 3 HP ports.

The side engine high pressure valves will be opened only if airframe anti−icing is on. Center engine HP valve will be opened if ENG 2 anti−icing is used.

NOTE: As long as HP2 valve is closed, the Pressure Regulating valve supplies with high pressure air the conditioning systems as required to make up low pressure air.

#### 1 ) Isolation valve

This valve is used for separating the common distribution line into two independent sub−systems.

- − The first receives the main LP/HP bleed air from the center engine and supplies the RH conditioning system as well as the center engine S duct air intake.
- − The other receives the main LP/HP bleed air from the side engines and supplies the LH conditioning and wing anti−icing systems. It also receives APU bleed air.
- − This valve is normally open. Air from any engine (and/or from the APU) supplies the system.
- − In the event of leak in either sub−system, the isolation valve can be closed to limit the leak to only one sub−system.
- − Especially, one conditioning system remains in use, which is sufficient for airplane pressurization and air conditioning.
- − This valve can also be used in case of smoke in the air conditioning system.

− Isolation valve control: rotate "ISOLATION" knob on the overhead panel by 90°.

In "OPEN" position, the blue bar on the knob is aligned with the system diagram visible on the panel.

#### 2 ) HP valves control

These valves are controlled by three 2−position switches (AUTO−OFF) located on the overhead panel and labeled HP 1, HP 2 and HP 3. OFF position is locked by a stud.

- − In AUTO, which is the normal position, the valve will open or close depending on associated anti−icing control switch position, on or off.
- − HP 1 and HP 3 valves: they will open when wing anti−icing is switched on (AIRFRAME−ON).
- − HP2 valve: it will open when engine 2 anti−icing is switched on (ENG 2 ON).
- − In OFF position, associated HP valve is closed whichever anti−icing control position is.

#### 3 ) Pressure regulating valve (PRV)

This valve is connected to engine 2 main HP bleed air port and directly supplies with regulated air pressure the two conditioning systems.

a ) Pressure regulation: the regulated pressure varies with the altitude.

| Altitude                         | 0                | 10,000 ft             | 25,000 ft             | 45,000 ft             |
|----------------------------------|------------------|-----------------------|-----------------------|-----------------------|
| Absolute<br>pressure<br><br>12 % | 2 bars<br>29 psi | 1.56 bars<br>22.6 psi | 1.12 bars<br>16.2 psi | 0.81 bars<br>11.8 psi |

#### b ) Operating instructions

The valve is controlled by a 2−position switch (AUTO−OFF) on the overhead panel, and labeled PRV.

- − Switch in OFF: the valve is closed in all cases (switch safetied by a stud).
- − Switch in AUTO (normal position)
  - If engine 2 anti−icing is in operation, the valve will automatically be closed for a more efficient use of HP2 air for anti−icing.
  - If engine 2 anti−icing is not in operation, the valve operates as follows:

The valve will deliver pressure when the pressure of the air supplying the conditioning systems is lower than or equal to the pressure controlled by the valve.

This corresponds to low engine rpm's (idle, descent rpm) where pressure is not sufficient enough to ensure proper operation of the air conditioning and pressurization systems.

#### Remark

If the low pressure supplied by the engines is much lower than that controlled by the PRV, the LP bleed air for the conditioning system will be null due to the miscellaneous check valves of the system.

If the low pressure is approximately the same as that controlled by the PRV, the air conditioned flow will be obtained by mixing normal LP bleed air and HP bleed air through the PRV.

### 4 ) BLEED OVHT light

This amber light is located on the failure warning panel and indicates an overheat in either bleed air system or at the inlet of either conditioning system. Such detection is achieved by 5 identical thermostats adjusted to 300°C (572°F) in increasing temperature, one by HP bleed air system and one by conditioning system. The 2 latter monitor the temperature of the air delivered by the PRV.

To identify faulty system: successively set HP 1, HP 2, HP 3 and PRV switches to OFF.

- − If the light remains on, reset concerned switch to AUTO and proceed with the following switch.
- − If the light is flashing on and off: The bleed air system associated to the last switch actuated is faulty.

Leave this switch off.

The light will extinguish when the system has cooled down to the thermostat threshold temperature (265°C/510°F) in decreasing temperatures.
