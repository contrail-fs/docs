#### 1 − GENERAL

#### A − Principle

- − The pressurization system controls the cabin pressure by actuating outflow valves that intercommunicate the cabin with the outside.
- − The pressurization system includes two outflow valves which control cabin air outflow in response to orders given by an automatic pressure regulator or by a manual control, in stand−by operation.
- − In stabilized flight, the air flowing out of the valves is equal to the air flowing into the cabin through the conditioning system.
- − The cabin is protected against overpressures by a device incorporated in the outflow valves.

The cabin is also protected against negative pressures:

- on airplane S/N's < 61 by negative pressure relief valves installed in the cold air supply system.
- on airplane S/N's - 61 by a device incorporated in the outflow valves. These outflow valves are equipped with a device which limits cabin pressure altitude in the event of failure or leakage.

The nose cone and the baggage compartment can be isolated from the cabin if necessary.

These two zones have their own safety devices against negative pressure and overpressure.

#### B − Rated pressure

The system is designed to maintain a maximum pressure−altitude of 8,000 ft in the cabin, at 45,000 ft which corresponds to a differential pressure (p) equal to 8.8 psi (607 mbar) or at 49,000 ft with a p equal to 9.15 psi (631 mbar), if F50−163 SB is incorporated.

In addition, each outflow valve is equipped with an overpressure relief safety device set to 9.15 psi (627 mbar), or to 9.5 psi (655 mbar) if F50−163 SB is incorporated.

#### C − Pressurization system description

The cabin pressurization system comprises two operating modes:

- − The normal (electropneumatic) mode called AUTOMATIC mode.
- − The stand−by mode (entirely pneumatic) called MANUAL mode.

PRESSURIZATION SYSTEM

#### 2 - NORMAL PRESSURIZATION SYSTEM

The normal system consists of:

- A cabin automatic pressure regulator.
- A normal pressure outflow valve actuated by the automatic pressure regulator.
- An emergency outflow valve.
- An antinicotin filter.

Normal system is set into operation by tilting the switch to AUTO (guarded position).

#### A - Automatic pressure regulator

- It is located on the RH lower portion of the center instrument panel; in the automatic mode, it generates electrical commands to the electropneumatic valve as function of the following data:
  - Commands set by the pilot.
  - Cabin pressure and rate-of-change of this pressure.
  - Airplane configuration (ground or flight and engine power setting on the ground).
- In the automatic mode, the emergency outflow valve follows
   –up the pneumatic solenoïd valve.

#### Automatic pressure regulator operation

#### 1) Commands from the crew

The commands are set by means of the controls provided on the instrument front panel.

- ALT knob: is used for selecting cabin altitude from -1,000 ft thru +10,000 ft of cabin pressure altitude.
- RATE knob: is used for selecting the pressure rate-of-change. To each position of this knob corresponds a value in increasing altitude and a value in decreasing altitude. A detent is provided and corresponds to:
  - +650 ft/min. up,
  - -450 ft/min. down.

The extreme position of the RATE knob gives in one direction a rate of climb of 200 ft/min. and a rate of descent of 100 ft/min.

In the other direction a rate of climb of 1,450 ft/min., a rate of descent of 1,000 ft/min.

#### − BARO knob:

This knob rotates the center calibrated dial to display QFE, QNH or a desired reference pressure. (Extreme value 29/31 in hg) in the window.

NOTE: This knob rotates the altitude display ring and not the pointer itself.

#### 2 ) Cabin altitude and cabin pressure rate−of−change detection

- − The regulator contains a pressure sensor that supplies cabin pressure information.
- − Pressure rate−of−change information is the derivative of the electronic pressure signal.

#### 3 ) In−flight operation

After comparison between cabin altitude and rate−of−change true and preset signals, the regulator sends commands to the electropneumatic valve torque motor to position the valves as desired.

- NOTE: − For deviations between preset and true cabin altitude higher than 100 ft, the rate−of−change will be set to the desired value by means of RATE knob.
  - − For deviations lower than 100 ft, the regulated rate−of−change will decrease as the altitude deviation is decreasing and then will remain between plus and minus 50 ft/mn.

#### 4 ) Operation on the ground

Airplane on the ground the altitude set by the crew is not taken into account by the regulator.

There are two cases:

- − If no throttle is above 54° FCU, the airplane is depressurized at the rate set by RATE knob. This means that the cabin is maintained or set to the ambient pressure (outflow valves fully open).
- − If either throttle is above 54° FCU, the regulator sends a command to increase cabin pressure at the rate set by RATE knob. As this position also causes the conditioning valves to close, there is no more air flow admitted in the cabin. As a result, the outflow valves will close but cabin pressure has not varied from its initial value.

#### B − Pressure control outflow valve

#### 1 ) General

#### Installation

The two outflow valves are installed on top of frame 28. The air flowing out of these valves is routed above the feeder tanks then through the zone surrounding the S−duct then out through louvers at fin stub.

#### Jet pump

This jet pump is installed in the aft compartment and provides a negative pressure of 35 mbar approximately necessary to the outflow valve operation. It is supplied with HP bleed air from engines 1 and 2, and by APU bleed air when the latter is installed.

#### 2 ) Electropneumatic control outflow valve

This valve is installed on the left side of the cabin and receives commands from the automatic regulator.

#### Description

#### − Main system

- The essential part of this valve is the mobile valve. Position of this valve will determine the opening section of the valve. It is connected to the fixed structure of the outflow valve body with a diaphragm.
- The inner face of the valve forms the pilot pressure chamber together with the valve body. The periphery of the diaphragm isolates this chamber from cabin pressure.
- The outer face of the valve is subjected to cabin pressure. It is isolated from outer pressure by the interior portion of the diaphragm.
- The valve is also subjected to the force of a spring tending to close it.
- In summary. the valve is subjected to:
  - .. A spring.
  - .. Cabin pressure.
  - .. Slaving pressure.
- The slaving pressure variations permit to pilot the outflow valve: the chamber is connected through two jets to the pressure of the cabin on one hand, to the negative pressure of the jet pump on the other hand. In automatic mode. a torque motor receives electrical signals from the automatic regulator and actuates a quadrant that caps off more or less the 2 jets,

thereby controlling the pilot pressure. thus controlling the balanced position of the outflow valve.

 In addition, a pipe connects the slaving chamber of the electropneumatic outflow valve to that of the slaved pneumatic valve.

#### Systems associated to the outflow valve

#### − Protection against overpressure

A pressure sensor is attached to the valve body and senses cabin pressure and static pressure.

When the p reaches 9.1 psi (627 mbar) or 9.5 psi (655 mbar) if SB F50−163 is incorporated the bellows expands and opens a pressure relief valve that allows the pilot pressure chamber to communicate with the static pressure. The pilot pressure therefore drops and tends to open the outflow valve: the air flow increases and cabin pressure will not exceed the preset value.

#### − "Cabin maximum altitude" protection

Another box containing a cabin pressure sensor is installed on the outflow valve. Whenever pressure altitude drops below 12,500 ft ( 1.500 ft) the aneroid capsule expands and opens a valve that allows the pilot pressure chamber to communicate with cabin pressure. The pilot pressure therefore augments and tends to close the outflow valve: the air flow decreases or is cancelled and cabin altitude becomes stable at 12,500 ft approx.

#### − Protection against negative pressure

When the outside pressure becomes higher than the cabin pressure, the protection against negative pressure allows the outside air to penetrate into the cabin through provided ports.

On airplane S/N's 61, the outflow valve is equipped with a negative pressure protection box.

Airplane S/N's < 61 are equipped with negative pressure relief valves which are independent, from the pressurization outflow valves.

These protections against overpressure. cabin maximum pressure, and negative pressure operate in the same way in automatic or manual mode.

#### − Antinicotin filter

This filter is installed only on the cabin air inlet to the electropneumatic outflow valve control chamber. It protects the chamber against air dirtying. due to nicotin.

#### 3 ) Emergency pneumatic outflow valve

This valve is mounted on the right side of the cabin.

#### − Main system

It is identical to the electropneumatic valve as far as the valve and pilot pressure chamber is concerned, but the torque motor is replaced by a pneumatic relay.

#### 3 − EMERGENCY PRESSURIZATION SYSTEM

- − Emergency system consists of:
  - An emergency manual regulator entirely pneumatic.
  - The emergency pneumatic outflow valve.
- − Operation is obtained by moving the switch to MAN.

NOTE: This selector switch cuts−off supply to the automatic regulator and returns the electropneumatic outflow valve torque motor to the non−operated position ; in this position the quadrant caps off the jet of the jet pump, thus closing the valve which will remain closed in manual regulation mode.

- In manual mode. the emergency outflow valve servo pressure is controlled by the pneumatic relay. This relay controls operation of a valve that more or less caps off the port setting into communication the slaving chamber with the jet pump.
- The pneumatic relay consists in a chamber in communication with the cabin, housing an aneroid capsule whose internal pressure is the reference pressure controlled by the manual regulator (this pressure is always lower than or equal to cabin pressure).
- The difference between cabin pressure and reference pressure will determine the position of the valve.

#### − Manual regulator

- It is located on the instrument panel, below the automatic regulator.
- It enables to ensure manual regulation of the pressurization by controlling the reference pressure that actuates the emergency pneumatic valve.

#### Display

- Pointer on position DN: −1,000 ft/min. (small green sector).
- Pointer on position UP: +1,500 ft/min.
- No cabin rate-of-change: pointer within the large white sector.
- Pointer at the stop, clockwise: 2,500 ft/min. approximately.

NOTE 1: In automatic control. the manual regulator should be set to DN.

NOTE 2: In the event of electrical power failure of the automatic regulator, the emergency outflow valve controlled by the manual regulator in DN position causes the cabin pressure to decrease at a rate of -1,000 ft/min.

#### Operation

The emergency regulator houses a chamber in communication with the 3 following pressures:

- 1. Pressure of the cabin through a restrictor.
- 2. Pressure of the jet pump, through a variable aperture port, controlled by a needle directly driven by the regulator knob.
- 3. Pressure of the reference volume through a capillary tube.
- In DN position, the needle entirely plugs off the jet pump. In such case, the regulator chamber is at the same pressure as the cabin. The reference pressure increases through the capillary which creates an air flow. The aneroid capsule of the relay expands, tending to close the outflow valve thus increasing cabin pressure. The rate-of-change is therefore negative. This rate-of-change will be stabilized since cabin pressure tends to compress again the relay aneroïd capsule.
- In UP position, the needle widely opens the port connecting the negative pressure to the regulator chamber: there is a depression in the chamber since the restrictor prevents the cabin air from flowing too quickly into it. Through the capillary, the reference pressure drops. The relay aneroid capsule is therefore compressed and tends to open the outflow valve, thus reducing cabin pressure. A negative rate-of-change is obtained. This rate-of-change is stabilized by cabin pressure which decreases thus tending to expand again the aneroid capsule.
- All rates-of-change between -1,000 ft/min. and +1,500 ft/min. may be set manually between UP and DN positions.
- There is especially a position which controls cabin pressure at a steady value (level flight). This position must be seeked on the large white sector between UP and DN.

#### 4 - "Rapid depressurization" (DUMP) function

There are two procedures allowing to rapidly depressurize the cabin:

A – By moving the switch to DUMP, this directly commands the torque motor to the "maximum depressurization" position, thus closing the cabin jet and fully opening the "negative pressure" jet.

Such procedures enables to retain the cabin "maximum altitude" protection; cabin altitude will stop at 12,500 ft.

However the torque motor must be operative and main bus A powered.

With this procedure the rates-of-change are very high.

B – By moving the switch to MAN and rotating the manual regulator knob fully clockwise (UP marking may be overshot). The rate–of–change obtained will be +2,500 ft/min. approximately.

#### PRESSURIZATION SYSTEM

#### 6 - BAGGAGE COMPARTMENT PRESSURIZATION

Baggage compartment pressure is regulated by intercommunication with the cabin; this compartment can be isolated by closing the intercommunication valve controlled by "BAG" switch on the overhead panel.

- In such case, hot air supply to the baggage compartment is shut-off per the following sequence:
  - 1° Closing of the hot air supply.
  - 2° Closing of the intercommunication.
- Opening sequence is the following:
  - 1° Opening of the intercommunication.
  - 2° Opening of the hot air supply.

The sequences prevent creating an excess pressure in the baggage compartment.

Refer to chapter Air conditioning.

#### Protections:

Two overpressure/negative pressure relief valves are installed at top of frame 35 between the baggage compartment and the aft compartment.

- Overpressure setting: +9.3 psi (+640 mbar).
- Negative pressure setting: -0.3 psi (-20 mbar).
- Overpressure setting: +9.71 psi (+670 mbar) if SB F50- 163 is incorporated.
- If the intercommunication valve is open, these safety devices supplement the cabin safety devices.
- If the intercommunication valve is closed, they maintain in the baggage compartment a pressure differential between -0.3 and 9.3 psi and the baggage compartment no longer benefits from cabin regulation.

#### 7 − NOSE CONE PRESSURIZATION

- − Since the nose cone houses many electronic equipment, is equipped with a ventilation system that operates on the ground and a pressurization/ conditioning system that operates in flight.
- − Air from the cabin enters the nose cone through a manually controlled valve (NOSE handle on the right console). This valve is normally open.
- − Pressure is regulated by controlling the amount of air flowing from the nose cone into the landing gear wheel well. This air is routed through a butterfly valve controlled by a pneumatic actuator that senses the pressure differential between cabin and nose cone.

#### On the ground:

Butterfly valve is fully open: there is no pressurization, but ventilation is obtained by a blower.

- In flight:
- − The blower is off.
- − The butterfly valve begins to close for a pressure differential of approximately 150 mbar (2.2 psi).
- − It is fully closed for an approximate p of 230 mbar (3.3 psi).
- − Air is still evacuated through two calibrated ports provided in the valve itself, maintaining in the nose cone a pressure exceeding the outer pressure by 60 mbar (.85 psi).

NOTE: The cabin air duct supplying the pneumatic actuator is also used for pressurizing the weather radar wave guide.

#### Pressure relief

A pressure relief valve opens whenever the cabin−nose cone pressure differential increases between 1.8 and 2.4 psi (124 and 166 mbar) above the outside pressure.

Should an abnormal air leak take place in the nose cone, that could affect cabin pressurization, the intercommunication valve must be closed using the NOSE handle.

#### 8 − PRESSURIZATION SYSTEM UTILIZATION

#### A − In AUTO mode

Before take−off:

- − Engines at idle (N1 < 54 %), or with the APU: the outflow valves are fully open whichever is the setting on AUTO regulator.
- − Settings for the flight:
  - BARO knob: if the departure airfield altitude is lower than 2,000 ft, set 29.92 in upon departure. If not, set the airfield QNH and set 29.92 in when the airplane reaches the Flight level 100.
  - RATE knob: the displayed cabin rate−of−change should enable to reach the selected cabin pressure before the airplane flight altitude is reached. The detent position corresponding to a rate−of−climb of 650 ft/mn covers most of the cases.

NOTE: This will prevent pressure relief from operating.

#### ALT knob

Is used for determining the cabin altitude to display on the regulator dial. In front of cabin altitude, are displayed the flight altitudes corresponding to the maximum outside−cabin pressure differentials (8.8 psi − 607 mbar).

In order to pressurize the cabin at a maximum Δp (for an optimal comfort) return the needle to the selected flight altitude (FLIGHT ALT scale). The cabin cruise altitude is read on CABIN scale.

Any cabin altitude comprised between the cabin altitude as determined above and 8,000 ft, can also be selected (Δp lower than 8.8 psi).

#### − At take−off

1st phase (airplane on the ground): N1 power setting of one engine at least above 54 %: the conditioning valves and the outflow valves close automatically.

2nd phase (take−off): the conditioning valves open and cabin rate−of−climb begins to rise.

NOTE: The setting of a flight altitude 1,000 ft higher than the flight true altitude improves the outflow valve operation.

#### B − Manual mode

- − Change from automatic mode to manual mode: it can be either voluntary (by setting the switch to MAN) or automatic (in case of regulator power failure).
- − When manual mode is set into operation, cabin rate−of−change becomes −1,000 ft/min. (DN position).
- − To avoid this variation in the case of a voluntary change, place the manual regulator on the white range (rate of change: null) and tilt the switch from AUTO to MAN.

This maneuver is mandatory if the automatic regulator does to operate properly in the abnormal CABIN rate of climb direction.

- − Descent: Set a rate−of−change of −450 ft/min. while initiating the descent to the destination airfield.
- − Carefully check cabin altitude reading on the triple indicator: if necessary, re−adjust the rate−of−descent so as to prevent cabin pressure from becoming lower than destination airfield pressure.

#### − Landing

Caution: there is no possibility of automatic depressurization on the ground.

After touch−down, manually set a positive rate−of−change of approximately 600 ft/min. (regulator knob at mid−range between white sector and UP position). This will open the outflow valves and establish balance between cabin pressure and outside pressure.
