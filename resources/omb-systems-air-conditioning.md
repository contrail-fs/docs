#### AIR CONDITIONING AND PRESSURISATION

The air conditioning and pressurization systems use compressed air furnished by the bleed air system.

The conditioning system controls cabin air temperature and renews this air.

The pressurization system controls the cabin pressure. It is designed to maintain a maximum pressure altitude of 8,000 ft (2,400 m) in the cabin up to 45,000 ft.

The nose cone is partially pressurized by interconnection with the passenger cabin.

The baggage compartment is pressurized by interconnection with the passenger cabin.

#### 1 − GENERAL

The air conditioning system comprises two identical sub−systems: one especially supplies the cockpit (RH system) the other the cabin (LH system).

They have two common parts:

- − The first is the main bleed air mixing duct, when the isolation valve is open. In such case the crew system is supplied from engine 2 and passenger system from engines 1 and 3 or by the APU (option).
- − The second is located at the meeting point between the hot air − cold air and the CABIN/CREW distribution assy. It is located at fr. 28, at head of the cabin air distribution system.

As a conclusion, the two air conditioning sub−systems can be totally independent from each other, in that portion outside the cabin.

However the intercommunication of the cabin air distribution system enables to have a correct cabin air conditioning with only one system in service.

Each sub−system is composed of two zones: the "Supply zone" which provides hot air and cold air and a "distribution" portion which mixes and properly distributes air in the passenger cabin and cockpit.

**SYSTEM DESCRIPTION** AIR CONDITIONING

#### B − Hot air/cold air

Cabin air conditioning is achieved by ducting a mixture of cold air and hot air into the distribution system, as controlled by the temperature control system.

Supply air is divided into two flows downstream of the limiter, one of cold air, the other of hot air. The dual temperature control valve varies the flows i.e. one butterfly is closing while the other is opening.

#### 1 ) Hot air

The hot air that flows through the "HOT" side of the double valve is ducted into the cabin. Duct is routed under the fuselage, goes in the wing−to−fuselage fairings and around the feeder tanks. The air enters the rear of the cabin through the landing gear well ceiling. Air under pressure is injected into the distribution system.

This creates a venturi which produces a negative pressure in the cabin, forcing cabin air into the distribution system and achieving a first mixing of hot air with cold air.

#### 2 ) Cold air

#### a ) Cold air generation

The hot air which flows through the "COLD" side of the double valve is cooled and expanded in the environmental control unit (ECU). The flow is never null since the deflection of the "cold" butterfly is limited to a minimum of 30% of the total flow. At ECU outlet the temperature is comprised between 14.5°C and 3°C.

- − 14.5°C corresponds to a pre−conditioning by engine 2, on the ground.
- − 3°C is the minimum value of the outlet air temperature controlled by a system internal to the cooling unit.

AIR CONDITIONING

- The cooling units perform also as water separators, so as to supply the cabin with low humidity. This is useful mainly at low altitude and on the ground, to prevent from supplying the cabin with haze when the outside air is damp. This also prevents the cooling units from icing.

NOTE: Under certain conditions (high outside temperature and humidity), ice spots may appear on the air outlets. This does not question the proper operation of the conditioning system.

#### Location

The two cooling units are installed in the aft compartment, on the fuselage right side. The upper unit is that of the cockpit system, the other of the cabin system.

#### b) Cold air system

After each cooling unit, the cold air is routed in a tubing at the top of the fuselage. Each tubing goes through the baggage compartment, above the feeder tanks and enters the fuselage at top of frame 28 when it is connected to the cabin air distribution system through a check valve.

#### 5 − CABIN TEMPERATURE CONTROL

There are two independent temperature control systems, one for the "passengers" zone, the other for the "crew" zone.

Controls and indications for the two systems are located on a single panel, on LH lower portion of the center instrument panel.

#### A − "Passengers" temperature control system

#### 1 ) Description

Temperature control is achieved by metering the amount of hot air and cold air injected in the cabin, by means of the double valve.

In automatic mode, the double valve is piloted by the temperature controller (selector in AUTO). It can be piloted manually (selector in MAN).

This system comprises:

- − A temperature probe mounted in the main RH "Cabin" conditioned air distribution line just downstream of the mixing and re−circulation system (duct probe).
- − A temperature probe in the passenger cabin aft headliner.

NOTE: These probes are resistors which vary with the temperature.

- − An electronic regulator built−in the cabin probe body.
- − A control and indication panel (RH part) including the "Automatic" and "Manual" controls, identified "PASSENGER".

#### 2 ) Automatic mode

 a ) Setting a selected temperature: It can be set by the white−dotted knob on the control panel. The full counterclockwise position corresponds to 18°C (65°F), the full clockwise position to 26°C (80°F). Total angular deflection of the knob (240°) permits to precisely set any temperature between these limits.

This knob actuates a rheostat that sends to the regulation amplifier a signal proportional to the selected temperature.

#### b ) Automatic regulation principle

- − Cabin temperature probe
  - The regulator compares the temperature sensed by the cabin temperature probe with the temperature selected potentiometer, by means of a wheatstone bridge.
  - The differential signal is amplified by the regulator and applied to the double conditioning valve which will move toward hot or cold position (main control signal).

#### − Duct probe

This is a dual probe housing two identical elements. One of them is heat insulated ; its temperature response therefore lags behind the other.

The two elements are connected to the regulator where they form two arms of a second wheatstone bridge. Differential signal is amplified and applied to the double conditioning valve (secondary control signal).

If the heat insulated element is colder than the other element, it means that the conditioned air temperature is increasing ; the secondary signal will tend to cause the conditioning valve to move toward a colder position.

#### − Temperature control operation

 The regulator combines the main and secondary signals and sends a command to the double conditioning valve under the shape of pulses, all the wider and closer to each other as the cabin temperature is away from the selected temperature. It is possible that the valve receives a permanent command to move in the required direction.

#### First case

Temperature selected is higher than the temperature sensed by the cabin probe. The double valve will move toward hot settings (main signal). If the command signal is high, the conditioned temperature will rise rapidly. The duct probe will sense a high temperature and will tend to damp the main command signal .

#### − 2nd case

From a stable position of the temperature control, let us assume that the engine rpm augments; the temperature of the hot air introduced in the conditioning system will increase and the temperature of the air conditioned will increase in turn. This will be detected by the duct probe and the regulator will cause the conditioning valve to move to the cold position. The temperature control by the cabin probe will not have to come into operation.

#### c ) Temperature control limitation

The automatic regulator also limits to 70°C (160°F) the maximum temperature of the air conditioned in the distribution system (temperature information is given by the duct probe element which is not heat insulated).

The double conditioning valve deflection is also limited in the hot direction; 30% at least of the total air flow is routed on the "cold" side.

#### 3 ) Manual mode of operation

In case of automatic system malfunction, it is possible to manually adjust the cabin air conditioned.

- a ) To select "Manual" mode: move the selector of the conditioning panel to "MANUAL". The automatic system is disabled and the COLD/HOT selector located below the selector is ready to operate (inoperative in automatic mode).

#### 6 − SYSTEM COMPONENT DESCRIPTION

- A − Conditioning valve: this valve is identical to the engine bleed air valves.
- B − Flow limiter.
- C − Double temperature control valve.
- D − Cooling unit: A cooling unit consists of:

#### 1 ) Component

- − A double air−to−air heat exchanger.
- − A detent turbine.
- − A blower driven by the turbine.
- − A system controlling output air temperature, consisting of a thermostat and a regulation valve.
- − A water condensation and evacuation system.

#### 2 ) Operation

#### a ) Cooling

The hot air from the engine (or APU) bleed ports is injected in the heat exchangers at lower portion of the cooling unit.

First heat exchanger: Cooling air blown by the fixed blower attached to the turbine shaft circulates in this exchanger.

Air is then sucked from outside through an air intake provided on the RH fuselage side. This intake is connected to the cooling unit by a box−type duct.

 Cooling air is evacuated outside through a circular outlet on RH fuselage side: the necessary negative pressure is obtained by a deflector.

The lower ECU air inlet duct is fitted with a sliding door installed immediately aft of the air intake, on the lower RH side of the fuselage. The door is normally held open by a latching pin connected by a cable to a control handle located in the rear RH side of the cabin.

Closure of this door prior to ditching is obtained by the release of two loading springs, when the control which frees the latching pin is pulled.

Second heat exchanger: The compressed air is then routed through a second heat exchanger in prolongation of the first one. The cooling air is pressure relieved cold air from the cooling unit outlet through a recirculation tubing.

Out of the exchanger, the air is re−injected in the cooling unit cold air outlet tubing by venturi effect created downstream of the expansion turbine.

NOTE: The second heat exchanger is also used as water condenser.

Turbine: Compressed air after the two heat exchangers is pressure relieved in a centripetal turbine where it is lastly cooled and provides the energy necessary to the cooling blower of the first heat exchanger (shafted to the turbine).

Venturi: Downstream of the turbine, air is injected in a venturi ending in the cold air outlet tubing. This venturi sucks in the re−circulation air.

#### b ) Temperature control

It is used for automatically maintaining the temperature of the air leaving the cooling unit to a minimum of 3 °C (37.5 °F).

Principle: A tubing by−passes a part of the hot air entering the heat exchanger to inject it directly downstream of the turbine output, through a regulation valve. This valve adjusts the by−pass flow and therefore the temperature of the air leaving the cooling unit.

Temperature control operation: Position of the temperature control valve depends on the pressure in the slaving chamber.

For a sensed temperature of 1 °C (33.8 °F) the temperature control valve is fully open. It is fully closed for a sensed temperature of 4 °C (39.2 °F).

 c ) Water collection system (operation is described for positive outside temperature conditions.

The conditioning air humidity is condensed at level of the second heat exchanger: air is cooled down to a temperature lower than the outside temperature. Water is therefore condensed on the exchanger output vertical blades. This water is collected at bottom of the exchanger and sprayed within the ventilation air of the first exchanger through two tubings, thus creating a permanent leak. This water spraying improves efficiency of the first heat exchanger.

 When the outside temperature is negative, this principle is useless (the air is already dry).

#### 8 − OPERATING INSTRUCTIONS

#### A − To set air conditioning systems into service

Use the 2 conditioning switches CABIN and CREW on the overhead panel.

- − Each is a three position switch:
  - OFF: The valve will close within 2 or 3 seconds (to isolate the system).
  - ON: The valve will rapidly open (within 2 or 3 seconds). Stand−by position used if AUTO should fail.
  - AUTO: This is the normal position of the switches. Position of the valve depends on the airplane configuration (ground or flight) and on engine rpm's.

If the power levers are in a position of less than 54 % N1, the valve is open. If switch is moved from OFF to AUTO the valve will open at a slow rate as described below.

- − If either of the three power levers is advanced to above 54 % N1, the valve will automatically close within 2 or 3 seconds. This especially prevents an abrupt increase in the conditioning air flow when take−off power is applied or if the thrust reversers are used.
- − If either throttle is then retarded to below 54 % the valves will open again at the fast rate.

#### C - Ground pre-conditioning on the ground by the APU

- Open the pilot window.
- BLEED AIR CREW and CABIN switches in AUTO (on overhead panel).
- CREW and PASSENGER switches in AUTO (on conditioning panel).
- Temperature selectors: at 12 o'clock.
- Start the APU.
- Move BLEED switch to ON.
  - When APU N1 reaches 95 %, APU bleed air valve will automatically open.
- Close the passenger door (slowly if possible).

#### D − Rapid pre−conditioning (by engine 2 or APU)

- − Open the small door situated at the corner of the sofa, next to the floor.
- − Air is directly admitted in the cabin through this door. The heated floors and zones below the floor are not supplied. This enables a very rapid conditioning of the cabin air (heating or cooling).
- − This small door must be closed prior to taxiing.
