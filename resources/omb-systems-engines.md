#### 1 − GARRETT TFE 731−3−1C ENGINE DESCRIPTION

#### A − General

The FALCON 50 airplane is equipped with 3 GARRETT AIRESEARCH TFE 731−3−1C two−spool front fan jet propulsion engines.

At take−off power setting, sea level−static conditions, 25°C (75°F), the engine thrust is 1,645 daN (3,700 lb), specific consumption 0.515 kg/kg/hr, dilution ratio 2.8 and compression ratio 14.6.

The engine is composed of an axial flow fan forcing air both to the gas generator low pressure compressor (primary air flow) and to a duct surrounding the gas generator (by−pass flow).

The primary air flow is successively routed through the 4 stage low pressure compressor, 1 stage high pressure compressor, through the annular combustor then is expanded in the HP turbine the 3 stage LP turbine and the primary nozzle.

The by−pass air flow compressed and accelerated by the fan is exhausted around the turbine hot gas flow. Such device allows, for an equal thrust, to reduce the gas exhaust speed thus improving the efficiency. Moreover the engine noise is reduced of 50 percent.

Engines with elliptical spinners are equipped with anti−icing systems using hot air. With a conical spinner (F50 0087 or GARRETT TFE 731−72−3085 SB) no anti−icing protection is required. However a simulation box installed, to restore signals to the lights and to retain PTR probe heating. Engines of both types (with conical or elliptical spinners) may be mounted on a same aircraft.

The LP compressor and the LP turbine are shafted to fan planetary gear assy, ratio of which is 0.5.

HP compressor is driven by the HP turbine through a hollow shaft concentrical with the LP spool shaft.

The HP spool drives the accessory gearbox. Motion is transmitted by a vertical shaft at 6 O'clock and a transfer gear assy.

The accessory gearbox drives the engine accessories (oil pumps, fuel pumps, fuel control) and has two drive pads: one 12,000 rpm pad is used by the starter−generator and the other 6,000 rpm pad is used by the hydraulic pump.

LP and HP spool speed is sensed by two phonical wheel sensor. N1 sensor is mounted on the aft bearing of the LP turbine and N2 sensor on the transfer gearbox.

The interstage temperature (ITT or TT5) is sensed by a 10 chromel−alumel thermocouple harness. Five of the thermocouples are connected to the computer and the other five to the associated indicator.

The engine is designed to operate with oils conforming to AIRESEARCH EMS 53 110 CLASS B specifications (Refer to GARRETT Operating Manual for the approved oils).

Oil consumption will not exceed 0.05 US gallon/h (0.19 l/h).

#### DESCRIPTION:

Lubricating oil is drawn by a HP pump from the oil tank located on the right flange of the engine and containing 1.5 US gal (5.6 l) of oil. There are two filler inlets, one on the tank, the other on the left side of the engine at the end of a line connected to the tank.

Oil level can be checked on a gage built−in each plug or through a window on the tank itself. A door is provided for this purpose in the center engine cowl and there are windows in the side engines cowls.

Downstream of the pump there is a pressure regulating valve and a pressure relief valve. The pressure regulating valve senses the fan supply pressure and maintains this pressure at 45 psi by by−passing more or less the HP pump inlet or outlet. It incorporates a valve that fully opens the by−pass whenever the pressure reaches 55 psi. The oil is then routed through a filter fitted with a by−pass valve that will open if the filter is clogged. There is also a visual indication consisting of a red colored button protruding outwards. This indicator can be checked by means of a small flap located on the lower nacelle cowl.

After leaving the filter, the oil flow is routed through a fuel heater also equipped with a by−pass valve, an oil−to−air heat exchanger, a temperature control and by−pass valve. The oil−to−air heat exchanger is a 3−segment cooler that forms the inner surface of the fan duct. Then one part of the oil passes through the oil−to−fuel heat exchanger equipped with a thermostatic valve that cools the oil if its temperature is too high. The oil is then routed to the fan reduction gearbox and front bearings (No 1, 2 and 3). The other part of the oil is directly routed to No 4−5−6 bearings and accessory drive gearbox through the transfer gearbox but is not routed through the oil−to−fuel heat exchanger.

Four scavenge pumps mounted on the same shaft as the HP pump shaft, force the oil back to the reservoir. A chip detector is mounted at the lower point of the reservoir return line.

System is vented through the accessory drive gearbox breather fitted with a control valve. This valve maintains a minimum pressure of 4 psi approximately in the accessory drive gearbox to prevent the pumps from cavitating.

#### Indication:

Oil pressure and temperature ports are provided downstream of the fuel−oil cooler. The fan reduction gearbox supply pressure and temperature is transmitted to an instrument reading these parameters on the instrument panel.

Should pressure in the system drop below 25 psi, a pressure switch will illuminate either light OIL 1 , OIL 2 or OIL 3 on the warning panel.

#### C − Ignition system

Ignition is achieved by discharge of a high−voltage interference−protected capacitor contained in the ignition box. This capacitor is supplied with 10 thru 30 VDC to provide the ignition plugs with 18,000 thru 24,000 V.

There are two plugs at 6 O'clock and 7 O'clock on the combustion chamber. They can provide 1 thru 5 sparks per second.

The circuits are supplied from primary bus A for engines 1 and 2 and primary bus B for engine 3. The circuit may function at continuous rating if flight conditions dictate it. Mode of operation is controlled by the three−position selectors on the overhead panel.

GRD START position : this is the position for a start on the ground. The ignition system is set into operation during the start sequence when the throttle of associated engine is advanced from stop position to idle. In such cases A and B primary busses are set in parallel with the start bus, by the start selector.

Ignition is automatically cut−off when N2 reaches 50 %.

Ignition system may also be energized by the stall warning system or STALL 1 and STALL 2 test buttons.

Ignition of the plugs is maintained for ten seconds after the test has been initiated or, in flight, after the angle−of−attack is again lower than 17°.

AIR START position, for in−flight relights (or any other condition requiring operation of the ignition system). Placing the selector in AIR START position will energize the ignition boxes and their relays as long as it remains in this position.

MOTOR START STOP : the ignition boxes cannot be energized. This position is used to cut−off ignition during an air start or for an engine motoring so as to evaporate excess fuel.

#### Fuel system description

The fuel is routed from the feeder tank of each engine to the engine through the feeder tank booster pump and fuel shut−off valves.

The fuel enters the engine fuel system at the inlet of the boost pump and through a 40 micron filter equipped with a bypass valve which will open in the event of blockage of the filter. At any time the bypass valve opens it actuates a pop−up red button, which can be checked during the post−flight inspection through a small flap located on the nacelle lower cowl.

Downstream of the filter the fuel passes through the anti−ice valve which controls the temperature of the fuel from the boost pump by directing one part of the fuel through the fuel heater mounted in the oil system to prevent the filter from icing.

The fuel is then routed through the high pressure pump. An encapsulated relief valve adjusted to 1,450 psi, between suction and pressure sides of the pump, permits to protect the system.

Fuel pump discharge enters the hydromechanical fuel control which meters the flow per the commands from the electronic computer (normal control) or from the pilot (manual mode).

Fuel from the fuel control enters the oil−to−fuel heat exchanger, the flowmeter then the flow divider. When fuel pressure reaches 10 psi, the primary atomizers are supplied and when the pressure differential between the primary atomizer flow and the secondary atomizer flow reaches approximately 60 psi, the latter will be supplied.

The primary and secondary fuel atomizers are concentrical. At low engine rpm, the 12 primary atomizers will spray fuel in a cone−shaped jet through the center orifice. At high rpm, the 12 secondary atomizers will spray a narrow jet which concentrates the primary jet.

The flowmeter is composed of an impeller driven by an alternating current motor which forces the fuel into a swirling motion. Downstream a second impeller linked to the first one by a torsion spring, is slaved to this swirling motion and takes a position which phase shift with the first wheel is proportional to the mass of fuel crossing the assy.

The HP pump delivers a fuel rated flow higher than that demanded by the engine at any setting. The hydromechanical fuel control limits the fuel flow to the engine. Excess fuel is returned to the inlet of HP pump and as required by the anti−ice valve, to the fuel heater.

#### D - Fuel control

Fuel control system is composed of:

- The hydromechanical fuel control that receives commands from:
  - The electronic computer (in normal mode).
  - The throttles (in manual mode).
  - P3 bleed air valves.
- An anti-cavitation valve controlled by the computer.

In addition to its metering section the fuel control shafted to the fuel pump comprises:

- An overspeed governor driven by the pump shaft. It senses the speed of HP spool and limits N2 to 105 % by varying the metering valve position through P3 limiter bellows.
- The throttle drive shaft. This shaft controls: a potentiometer which provides the computer with throttle position data, the fuel shut-off valve and a cam that varies the adjustment of the overspeed governor in manual mode. In such case this device is used as speed governor.
- A P3 limiter bellows to protect the engine.
- A mode selector solenoïd valve (normal or manual mode). This valve is normally excited open by the computer. The fuel pressure is exerted on the slaving piston that is thus locked in extreme position. The speed governor will operate at 105 % N2. When the computer is isolated through CMPTR switch, the solenoïd valve will close, the piston will come in contact with the throttle cam and the fuel control will operate fox the commanded N2 %.
- A fuel shut-off solenoid valve operating in case of overspeed. This valve is controlled by the computer and shuts off fuel flow whenever N1 or N2 exceeds 109 %.
- An adjustment screw of the maximum thrust in manual mode.

The metering section of the fuel control operates as follows: Aperture of the metering valve depends on P3 pressure. When this pressure rises, (for example when the engine is started) a differential bellows causes the valve to open.

In normal mode, the computer sends a signal to a torque motor that establishes a certain  $\Delta P$  by more or less bypassing the input and output of the hydromechanical fuel control. Therefore, engine rpm (thus P3) tends to be stabilized and maintains the metering valve to the proper position. The computer then returns the torque motor to its non-operated position and a differential pressure bellows will maintain a LP of 40 thru 45 psi across the metering valve.

In manual control, the action of the torque motor is limited. The throttle, thanks to the mode selector solenoïd valve, controls the setting of the hydromechanical fuel control through a cam. When the engine has reached the required N2, the fuel control will create a P3 bypass until N2 is stabilized.  $\Delta P$  across the metering valve is maintained to 40–45 psi by the  $\Delta P$ bellows.

Engine 2 electronic computer is mounted in the rear compartment and the others in the baggage compartment. The computers are energized by CMPTR 1, 2 and 3 switches on overhead panel. The internal circuits and the data provided by the computers are monitored. Should a defect be detected, the associated computer wall be cut-off immediately and manual mode is established. Such cutting off whether voluntary or not, is indicated by associated | CMPTR | light on failure panel.

The computer receives the following input signals:

- Throttle angle through the fuel control potentiometer.
- Engine inlet pressure and temperature (Pt2 and Tt2).
- LP (N1) and HP (N2) spool speed.
- ITT temperature.

Fuel control in normal mode (with the computer)

For a given position of the throttle, the computer controls N2 by the ratio  $\left(\frac{WF}{P3}\right)^2$  as function of Pt2 and Tt2 (WF is the demanded flow). This ratio controls the torque motor.

This fuel flow will be reduced if one of the monitored limits is exceeded. In the event of LP or HP spool overspeed at 109 %, the fuel flow is canceled by closing of the overspeed solenoïd valve controlled simultaneously by two independent circuits.

During the start or an in-flight relight, the computer controls the fuel flow so as to limit TT5. An internal circuit of the computer increases the fuel flow during the initial take-off phase.

- − Acceleration−deceleration: in the event of computer failure, the antipumping valve is 1/3 open. Abrupt throttle changes should therefore be avoided since this valve cannot open fully.

#### E − Engine synchronization (Option 77−33−01)

Engine synchronization system is designed to synchronize the HP or LP spool rotation speeds to prevent beating that could disturb passengers' comfort.

Synchronization may be achieved, upon crew's choice, for the HP spools (N2) or the fan spools (N1).

This system consists of two synchronizers, each associated to one side engine, adjusting their N1 or N2 to that of the center engine.

The engine computers include the provisions for this system.

Synchronization is controlled by one two−position switch (ON−OFF) located on the pedestal.

A 2−position selector, N1−N2 is used for selecting either HP spool or fan spool speed synchronization.

Two circular indicators with a pointer that moves to the left or to the right of SYNCH index according to whether the slaved engine runs slower (−) or faster (+) than the center engine.

#### Operation:

N1 or N2 signal of either side engine and of the center engine are sent to a comparator monitor. This output signal is amplified and applied to the side engine computer via an authority limiting circuit.

This circuit ensures on one hand that the slaved computer receives only a command for a limited correction of the power setting and on the other hand, that it is efficient only within a given rpm range. For example, the synchronizers' signal is zero when the throttle is at idle or take−off setting. These power settings can always be obtained by the throttle and cannot, because of the synchronizer, be lowered to less than the computed values.

Synchronization system is automatically cut−off whenever either side engine or the center engine are running in manual mode.

The engine synchronization and indication functions being independent allow the crew to be informed on the synchronization status, therefore the crew can:

- − Adjust engine rpm's by means of the throttles prior to engaging synchronization function.
- − Check synchronization system for correct operation.
- − Manually synchronize the engines should either one run in manual mode.

#### 2 − ENGINE INSTALLATION

#### Side engines

The side engines are attached in three points. Two front mounts withstand whole engine thrust and the rear mount carries the vertical and side loads in order to enable longitudinal thermal expansion of the engine.

The nacelle is ventilated to prevent overheating on flammable vapor concentrations. Drain ports are provided to prevent fluid accumulation.

#### Center engine

Center engine is mounted by two front mounts and an aft auxiliary mount. The mounts are mounted to vibration isolators.

The hoist fitting forms a bridge over the attach points. The manually controlled hoist is located in the fin stub. The hoist fitting has two points allowing to hoist either engine only or engine with aft cone and thrust reverser. Hoist can be reversed so that the cable faces the associated point.

The conical spinner (F50 0087 SB or GARRETT TFE 731−72−3085 SB applied) is longer than the elliptical spinner.

A special tool is necessary to back−up engine prior to hoisting it.

Alternately, the spinner can be removed prior to hoisting engine.

The nacelle of this engine is also ventilated and drained.

#### 3 − CENTER ENGINE THRUST REVERSER

The thrust reverser is installed on the center engine. The target doors are hinged to a vertical pin. In cruise configuration, the 2 doors surround the aft fan duct. In opened position, they reverse the air flow in a horizontal direction and rest against each other by their aft frame.

Efficiency of the thrust reverser is approximately 40 % of the take−off thrust. Such thrust can be set up to IAS = 0 with no risk of exhaust gas re−ingestion. Reverser deployment or stowing is achieved within 2 seconds. A stop limits to 93% the N1 reached with the thrust reverser in operation.

Operation: The hydraulic control actuator actuates a bellcrank which drives two rods each controlling one door. The main latch constitutes a grill built−in the hydraulic actuator. A S−shaped hook actuated by a solenoïd, serves as auxiliary latch. Thrust reverser is supplied by airplane hydraulic system 1 through an accumulator which will ensure at least one complete maneuver of the reverser (deployment−stowage), within three hours following a failure of system 1.

CAUTION: ANY THRUST REVERSER OPERATION ON THE GROUND SHOULD BE CARRIED OUT UNTIL THE DOORS ARE COMPLETELY STOWED, AS INDICATED BY EXTINCTION OF THE LIGHTS.

A four−way electro−distributor supplies the actuator. The actuator ensures stowing of the reverser up to IAS = 180 kt.

− Thrust reverser deployment sequence:

The thrust reverser control is hinged to the center engine throttle (piggy back throttle). The electrical circuit and the control is so designed that the reverser can be deployed only if the two following conditions are complied with simultaneously:

- Airplane on the ground. This energizes the squat switches.
- Engine throttle at idle (this position allows the mechanical latch of the piggy back throttle to be released).

The initial travel of the piggyback throttle is limited by an electrical stop but is sufficient to allow the auxiliary latch excitation control microswitch to close. When this latch is open, the electro−distributor is energized to deploy the reverser and TRANSIT light comes on.

The hydraulic pressure in turn unlocks the actuator internal latch dogs thus deploying the doors and locking the throttle by means of an electrical latch.

#### Flight with inoperative thrust reverser

It is possible to fly with an inoperative thrust reverser provided that:

- − It is not damaged in any way.
- − It is deactivated and pinned in the stowed position.

To pin the thrust reverser in the stowed position:

- − Check that the doors are stowed and mechanically locked.
- − Insert the locking pin in the appropriate hole (upper position of the reverser), check that this pin latches correctly and does not catch on the other holes (to ensure that the correct hole is used).

To deactivate the thrust reverser control, proceed as follows:

- − Pull the CONTROL REVERSE circuit breaker on the center circuit breaker panel in the cockpit.
- − Attach a lable on the THRUST REVERSER switch safety guard to advise the crew that the thrust reverser is inoperative.

NOTE: In case of prolonged use of the airplane with an inoperative thrust reverser, the pin can be replaced by a bolt.

CAUTION: AFTER REPAIR DO NOT FORGET TO REINSTALL RED PENNANT ON PIN.

#### 4 − ABNORMAL ENGINE OPERATION

#### Engine motoring

Retard the throttle to stop and set the start selector to MOTOR−START STOP.

The fuel supply and ignition circuit are not activated.

The engine may be started by pressing the start pushbutton.

However running the engine in this configuration, for approximately 2 minutes, could result in the oil from the accessory gearbox overflowing through the drains.

Therefore, this procedure is limited to 15 seconds, followed by 5 minutes stop.

#### Failures upon starting

#### − Total start time

The engine should not take more than 50 seconds to accelerate up to the idle rpm. Should this time be exceeded, interrupt the start.

− No acceleration of the HP spool (N2)

If N2 speed does not increase steadily and fastly up to 24 % since the beginning of ignition, interrupt the start.

− N1 is still zero whereas N2 has reached 20 %

Interrupt the start.

− ITT does not rise

Interrupt the start.

− Temperature limit is exceeded

If ITT is rapidly rising close to the limit, immediately retard the throttle to stop. Exceeding the ITT limit may be detrimental to the engine. Set then the start selector to MOTOR−START STOP.

− No oil pressure

If the oil pressure has not risen 10 seconds after the beginning of ignition, interrupt the start.

#### Multiple start attempts

NOTE: Only attempt another start if ITT has dropped back to below 200 °C.

After an unsuccessful start attempt which besides did not exceed a duration of 40 seconds, proceed as follows:

- − After one minute stop, perform a motoring for not more than 15 seconds followed by 2 minutes stop at least.
- − Proceed with another start attempt; interrupt it if the idle is not reached within 30 seconds (instead of 40 seconds for the first attempt).
- − If unsuccessful, allow another minute stop and perform a second motoring limited to 15 seconds, followed by 2 minutes stop.
- − Perform a third and last start attempt (for 30 seconds). If unsuccessful, interrupt the start.
- − Maximum number of start attempts for the same engine: 3.
- − Minimum cooling down time after this cycle: 30 minutes.

During this time, try to locate the fault.

NOTE: The delay between two successive start attempts should be multiplied by 2 in hot weather conditions (temperature above 30 °C).

#### In−flight flame−out

See section 3. Emergency and abnormal procedures.

#### Engine failure

Instrument integrity being assumed, failure symptoms can be:

- − Vibrations.
- − An abrupt change in oil pressure reading, at a given power setting.
- − A rapid rise in oil temperature.
- − Abnormal N1 or N2 reading.
- − ITT limit exceeded (it shall be returned to normal by reducing power and, if necessary, shutting down the engine).
- − Abnormal increase in fuel flow for a set thrust and a given outside temperature.

Should a failure occur, the engine must immediately be isolated as far as electrical and fuel supplies are concerned. Damages should not ensue since the compressor continues to motor. If engine thrust drops due to fuel in excess or to a stall, an in−flight relight may be attempted.

Do not attempt a relight after an engine fire or if engine integrity is questioned.

#### Engine fire

Refer to fire protection chapter.

#### Either throttle break−down

Engine rpm does not change, or slightly changes. Shut down the engine by means of the fuel shut off valve.

#### Computer failure

This subject is dealt with in step D: Fuel control.

It is reminded that:

- − The ITT temperature of the other engines controlled by their computers must not been exceeded on that engine.
- − Avoid abrupt movements of the throttles.
- − Above 20,000 ft, do not reduce N1 below 70 %.
- − At idle, the thrust is higher.
- − The take−off thrust is reduced of approximately 20 %.
