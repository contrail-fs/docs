HYDRAULIC POWER

#### 1 - Description

This airplane is equipped with two independent hydraulic systems, with no possibility of interconnection between each other.

- The hydraulic fluid authorized for use must conform to MIL-H-5606 Specification (NATO codes: H515 or H520).
- Hydraulic power is provided in normal mode by three self-regulating pumps driven by the accessory gear box of each engine.
- Each pump has a maximum rated flow of 20.3 I per min. and delivers a regulated pressure of 206 bars (3,000 psi) which is the normal pressure of the two systems with no flow.
- Emergency hydraulic power (system 2) is produced by a stand-by electric pump.
- On the ground, each system can be supplied from a ground power unit or by the stand-by pump.
- Pressure of each system is measured by means of a pressure transmitter and can be read on the right scales of the two pressure/quantity indicators (located in the middle of the hydraulic panel).
  - Green range: from 2,800 thru 3,000 psi or from 2,800 thru 3,200 psi depending on type of equipment

#### System 1

System 1 is powered by the two pumps mounted on the center and the left engine and supplies through a surge-damper accumulator the following utilities.

- Rudder, elevator and aileron servo-actuator system 1 barrels.
- Elevator and aileron Arthur Q units.
- Inboard and outboard slat normal control system.
- Thrust reverser control system.
- Landing gear operation control systems.
- Normal braking system.

#### System 2

This system is powered by the pump mounted on the right engine and, in emergency, by a stand-by pump.

#### 2 − OPERATION − CONTROLS AND INDICATION

A − Hydraulic reservoirs (there are two identical reservoirs, one per system)

In the reservoirs:

- − Fluid is sucked by the pumps.
- − Fluid returns after utilization.

Both reservoirs are located in the aft compartment.

#### 1 ) Pressurization

The reservoirs are pressurized at 1.47 bar (21.3 psi) by air from engine 1 and 2 LP bleed ports, through a pressure reducing valve. The air enters each reservoir through a pressurization valve assy including:

- − The inlet valve.
- − A pressure relief valve opening at 1.8 bar (26 psi).
- − A negative pressure relief valve calibrated to 27 relative mbar (0.4 psi).
- − A pressure switch adjusted to 1.1 absolute bar (16 psi) decreasing pressure, controls illumination of TK.P1 or TK.P2 amber light on hydraulic panel, indicating that corresponding reservoir is depressurized.
- − The pressurization will prevent cavitation upon suction of the hydraulic fluid.
- − Should either light illuminate, monitor the fluid quantity/pressure indicators. If pressure begins to fluctuate, descend to an altitude below 20,000 ft as soon as conditions permit.

#### 2 ) Capacity − Gages

The maximum usable capacity in each reservoir is approximately 12 liters and the volume up to the filling level is 9.85 liters.

− Hydraulic fluid level in the reservoir can be seen on the ground through a window on the side of the reservoir. This allows to complete filling before the flight, if necessary. In this case, the reservoir depressurization is manually made through the filling port.

- Fluid gaging system consists of a float valve and a potentiometer transmitter.

Fluid level is indicated on the left scale of each of the double pressure/level indicators on the hydraulic panel.

There is a red range between MINI and 1/4 and a green range from 5/8 to MAX. For indicators amendment C and up, the green range extends of 4 mm beyond MAX.

#### – NOTE: In system 1:

- The fluid level drops of approximately 13 mm when system is set under pressure, which is the quantity of fluid stored in the accumulators (main accumulator and thrust reverser accumulator).
- The fluid level rises of approximately 41 me while the landing gear is retracting, due to the disymmetry of the actuating cylinder chambers. This phenomenon is reversed upon extension of the landing gear: level drops of 41 mm (slightly more than 1/4 of division on the indicator).

#### In system 2:

 Fluid level drops of approximately 20 mm when system is set under pressure, due to the accumulators (main accumulator in system 2 and parking/emergency brake accumulator).

#### 3) Filtering

- The filling port is equipped with a filter.
- The suction canister is equipped with a filter.

#### B - Main pumps

#### 1) Description

- They are of the self-regulating barrel-type.
- They deliver a regulated pressure of:
  - 206 bars (3,000 psi) for zero flow.
  - 197 bars (2,850 psi) for maximum flow.

HYDRAULIC POWER

- The maximum rated flows proportional to engine N2 (20.3 I per min. at 100 % N2).
- There are no controls for the pumps in the cockpit but there is a fuse on the pump shaft to protect the engine in the event of pump jamming.

#### 2) Indication

Pressure delivered by each pump is monitored by a pressure switch which illuminates
 PMP 1 , PMP 2 or PMP 3 lights whenever the pressure delivered by the associated pump drops below 103.5 bars (1,500 psi).

NOTE: PMP 1 and PMP 2 lights may come on while extending the gear with engines 1 and 2 at idle. This indicated drop in pressure is normal during the extension cycle.

- Lights will extinguish when pressure rises to 150 bars (2,150 psi).

#### C – Stand-by pump system

The stand-by pump is driven by an electrical motor and delivers a rated flow of 4.5 liters per min. It is installed in the aft compartment.

- In flight, the stand by pump is connected to system 2.
- On ground, a manual selector installed in the aft compartment on the RH side can be used, it has two positions:
  - IN-FLIGHT (system 2 supplied in flight or on ground) This position is the normal configuration for take-off. The selector is normally safetied in this position.
  - GROUND-TEST: This position is used for ground testing of system 1 and ancillary hydraulic equipment in the absence of a ground hydraulic power unit.

#### 1) Operation

Stand-by pump activation depends on the following:

- System 1 or system 2 pressure by means of a pressure switch located downstream of the accumulator of the system concerned.
  - Drop in pressure in the corresponding system below 103.5 bars (1,500 psi).
  - Stand-by pump will automatically stop when pressure rises above 150 bars (2,150 psi).
- The position of the ST BY PUMP control lever located on the right side of the hydraulic panel (AUTO, ON, OFF).
- The airplane configuration (on ground or in flight).

The stand-by pump is supplies system 2.

#### a ) Normal operation:

Selector in IN−FLIGHT position

(The stand by pump is connected to system 2)

- − Control lever set to ON or AUTO
  - - On the ground: If the pressure in system 2 has fallen below the pressure switch setting 1.500 psi, (10.5 bars), the stand−by pump is automatically activated and stops when the pressure reaches 2,150 psi (150 bars).
- − Control lever set to AUTO
  - - In flight: When the airbrake control handle is set to a position other than "airbrakes retracted", if the pressure in system 2 falls below the pressure switch setting 1,500 psi, (103.5 bars), the stand by pump is automatically activated and stops when the pressure reaches 2,150 psi (150 bars).
- − Control lever set to ON
  - - In flight: the stand by pump is activated. The regulation is controlled by a pressure switch according to the generating system to which the pump is connected.

#### b ) Operation for ground tests

- − Selector set to GROUND−TEST (the stand by pump is connected to system 1 and the ST PUMP light is permanently illuminated).
  - The stand by pump cycles between 1,500 and 2,150 psi when the ST BY PUMP control lever is set to ON or AUTO.

NOTE: When the manual selector set to GROUND−TEST, the ST. PUMP light is permanently illuminated indicating that the selector is not in the normal take−off configuration.

CAUTION: THIS MANUAL SELECTOR MUST NOT UNDER ANY CIRCUMSTANCES BE OPERATED WHEN THE STAND BY PUMP IS RUNNING.

#### 2 ) Indication

- − An amber ST.PMP light illuminates:
  - When the stand by pump operating tune exceeds 60 seconds (should this light come on in flight, move lever to OFF to stop the stand by pump.
  - As soon as the selector is set to the GROUND−TEST position: In this configuration the light is permanently illuminated.

NOTE: There is no light indicating that the stand by pump is activated.

Stand by pump test: The stand−by pump must be tested before each flight as follows: With engine 3 shutdown, set control lever to AUTO and check pressure in system 2 for cycling between 2,150 and 1,500 psi. Such test is part of the normal procedures of the Flight Manual.

#### D − Accumulators

Each hydraulic system includes an oleopneumatic accumulator used also as surge damper.

#### Characteristics:

- − Maximum air volume: 820 cu.cm (50 cu.in).
- − Inflating pressure on the ground: 100 bars (1,450 psi).
- − Hydraulic fluid volume: 420 cu.cm (25.7 cu.in) at 207 bars (3,000 psi).

Each accumulator is equipped with:

- − An inflating valve.
- − A pressure gage graduated in psi.

NOTE**:** −System 1 has an additional accumulator used by the thrust reverser system.

> −System 2 has also an additional accumulator used by the parking and emergency braking system.

#### 3 − NORMAL OPERATION

#### A − Prior to starting the engines

- − Check that reservoir level indication is within the green range: all the systems can be used normally without risk for the level to drop in the red range.
- − Test the stand−by pump and lights (check−list procedure).

#### B − While taxiing

Check during taxiing that the levels are correct after maneuvering the flight controls (no leaks).

#### C − Before approach−landing

Check for correct levels for a normal approach and landing (minimum: 1/2 scale).
