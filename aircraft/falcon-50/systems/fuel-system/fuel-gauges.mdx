Three fuel quantity gauges <Badge color="yellow">2</Badge> are located in the center instrument panel, one for each fuel subsystem (left, center, right). A single **TOT–REAR selector switch** <Badge color="yellow">3</Badge> controls all three gauges simultaneously.

## Fuel Flow Gauges

Three fuel flow gauges <Badge color="yellow">1</Badge> are also located on the center instrument panel, one for each engine. Fuel flow is sensed by a **mass flow transmitter** on each engine and displayed on the associated gauge.

Each gauge shows instantaneous fuel flow on a circular dial calibrated from 0 to 2,200 **PPH** (pounds per hour). The indication is accurate only when flow exceeds approximately **150 PPH**. A drum counter window displays cumulative fuel consumed by that engine, from 0 to 9,990 lb in 10 lb increments. Pull the front-panel knob to reset the counter to zero.

A fuel flow totalizer and aircraft weight indicator <Badge color="yellow">4</Badge> is also installed.

## Gauge Layout and Selector

The selector has two positions that change what all three gauges display at once:

| Position | Display | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **REAR** | Feeder tank quantity only | Normal operating position. Shows only the feeder tank quantity. |
| **TOT** | Total subsystem fuel | Shows forward tank plus feeder tank for each subsystem (left, center, or right). |

In **REAR**, each gauge indicates only the fuel in its feeder tank — typically the regulated 609 lb during the cruise phase. In **TOT**, each gauge indicates the combined quantity of the feeder tank plus its associated forward tank (wing tank, or for the center subsystem, the center wing plus center feeder tank).

## Reading and Monitoring

Fuel quantity must be checked periodically during flight to confirm it is decreasing as expected. This check is performed by switching the selector to **TOT** from time to time and comparing readings against fuel burn projections.

When a forward tank is empty, the gauge in the **TOT** position will read **609 lb** for that subsystem — this represents the regulated quantity remaining in the feeder tank.

<Note>
  The fuel quantity indicators may show slightly higher values than the quantity actually loaded. The difference can be as much as 200 to 300 pounds with 3,000 pounds in one set of tanks (a wing tank, or the center wing plus its associated feeder tank).
</Note>
