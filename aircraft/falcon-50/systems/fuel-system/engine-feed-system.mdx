---
title: "Engine Feed System"
---

The engine feed system supplies fuel from the feeder tanks to the engines under all normal and abnormal operating conditions. Each engine has a dedicated feeder tank, with crossfeed available for emergency configurations.

## Feeder Tanks

Three feeder tanks are installed in the rear fuselage, one per engine. The fuel system automatically maintains each feeder tank at a nominal **609 lb** during flight: above this regulation level, transfer from the forward tanks is inhibited until quantity decreases to 609 lb; below it, fuel is automatically drawn from the associated forward tank. When remaining fuel is insufficient to maintain regulation, the feeder tank quantity decreases normally.

| Feeder Tank | Maximum Capacity |
|-------------|------------------|
| **Left Feeder Tank** | ≈ 1,400 lb |
| **Center Feeder Tank** | ≈ 2,460 lb |
| **Right Feeder Tank** | ≈ 1,400 lb |

<Note>
The 609 lb regulation level provides a buffer of fuel in each feeder tank, ensuring continuous engine feed even during transfer pump cycling or brief interruptions in fuel transfer.
</Note>

## Booster Pumps

Each feeder tank contains a **booster pump** that supplies positive-pressure fuel to its engine-driven fuel pump. Engines depend on this booster pump for normal operation; if pressure downstream of a booster pump drops, the corresponding **FUEL** warning illuminates.

<Warning>
Booster pump failure results in loss of positive-pressure fuel supply to the engine. The FUEL warning will illuminate, and crossfeed may be required to maintain engine operation.
</Warning>

## Crossfeed Valves

Crossfeed valves allow one feeder tank to supply more than one engine. They are normally closed during standard operations and are used following a booster pump or feeder tank failure to permit continued engine operation from a single feeder tank.

<Warning>
Crossfeed operations should only be used during abnormal conditions. Monitor fuel quantities closely when using crossfeed, as a single feeder tank will then supply multiple engines.
</Warning>
