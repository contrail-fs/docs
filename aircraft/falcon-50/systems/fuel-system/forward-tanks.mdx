---
title: "Forward Tanks"
---

The Falcon 50 forward tanks provide **fuel storage only** — they do not supply fuel directly to the engines. All engine feed occurs through the feeder tanks, which receive fuel from the forward tanks via the transfer system.

## Tank Arrangement

The fuel system includes three forward tanks arranged in a left-center-right configuration. Each wing tank is subdivided into outboard and inboard sections by internal structural baffles, which support controlled fuel distribution within the wing.

| Tank | Location | Usable Fuel Capacity |
|------|----------|----------------------|
| **Left Wing Tank** | Left wing | 3,748 lb |
| **Center Tank** | Center fuselage | 2,749 lb |
| **Right Wing Tank** | Right wing | 3,748 lb |

Total forward tank capacity is **10,245 lb**.

## Wing Fuel Distribution

Within each wing tank, fuel is moved from the outboard sections to the inboard sections so that outboard (more aft) fuel is consumed first. This pattern shifts the aircraft's center of gravity progressively forward as fuel burns, improving longitudinal stability and handling characteristics.

The redistribution is performed by **wing jet pumps**, which use the venturi principle and are powered by excess fuel flow from the transfer pumps. If a transfer pump is inoperative, fuel still moves inboard by gravity through **flapper valves**, ensuring outboard fuel continues to drain regardless of pump status.

<Tip>
For detailed jet pump operation, see the [Fuel Transfer System](/aircraft/falcon-50/systems/fuel-system/fuel-transfer-system) section.
</Tip>

## Pressurization

All forward tanks are pressurized by **low-pressure bleed air from Engines 1 and 2**, automatically whenever either engine is operating. The forward tanks are maintained at a higher pressure than the feeder tanks, which establishes a differential pressure that drives fuel toward the feeder tanks independently of the transfer pumps.

<Note>
The pressurization system provides a backup transfer mechanism. Even if all transfer pumps fail, fuel continues to flow from the forward tanks to the feeder tanks as long as engine bleed air pressurization is maintained.
</Note>
