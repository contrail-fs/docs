---
title: "Fuel Transfer System"
---

The fuel transfer system moves fuel from the forward tanks to the feeder tanks and manages fuel distribution within the wing tanks. Two complementary transfer paths — electrically driven pumps and tank pressurization differential — work in parallel for redundancy.

## Transfer Mechanisms

Each subsystem is equipped with an electrically driven **transfer pump** in its forward tank. The transfer pumps provide the primary means of moving fuel from forward tanks toward feeder tanks, and they also supply the excess fuel flow and pressure that drives the wing jet pumps. Transfer pumps are normally switched off once the associated forward tank is empty.

In parallel, fuel flows from the forward tanks toward the feeder tanks under **differential pressure** created by tank pressurization, with no dependence on the transfer pumps. Fuel can therefore continue to reach the feeder tanks even when the transfer pumps are off or have failed.

<Tip>
This dual-path design ensures continued feeder tank replenishment without sole reliance on electrical pumps. Even after a transfer pump failure, fuel continues to flow toward the feeder tanks as long as pressurization is maintained.
</Tip>

## Wing Jet Pumps

Two wing jet pumps are installed, one in each wing. These venturi-type pumps transfer fuel from the **outboard wing section to the inboard wing section** so that outboard fuel is burned first, supporting center-of-gravity management.

The left wing jet pump is powered by excess fuel flow and pressure from **Transfer Pump No. 1**, and the right wing jet pump by **Transfer Pump No. 3**. The jet pumps run whenever their associated transfer pump runs, and they do not feed the feeder tanks directly. If the transfer pump fails, fuel continues to move from outboard to inboard sections by gravity through flapper valves.

## Transfer Interconnect Valves

Two transfer interconnect valves, controlled from the overhead fuel panel, allow fuel from any forward tank to be routed to any feeder tank as required. Check valves in the system prevent unintended lateral fuel transfer between wing tanks.

<Tip>
Use the interconnect valves to balance fuel distribution or to route fuel from a full tank to a feeder tank when its own associated forward tank is empty.
</Tip>
