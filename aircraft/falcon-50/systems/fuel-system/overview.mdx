The Falcon 50 fuel system supplies all three engines through a **feeder-tank architecture** with automatic quantity regulation, multiple transfer paths, and built-in redundancy. The system is arranged as three normally independent subsystems — left, center, and right — with cross-tie capability for abnormal operations.

<Note>
Engines are never supplied directly from wing or center tanks. All engine feed occurs exclusively through feeder tanks, each of which is automatically regulated to **609 lb** during flight.
</Note>

## System Architecture

Fuel is stored in three forward tanks (left wing, center, right wing) and supplied to the engines through three feeder tanks in the rear fuselage. Each engine draws fuel exclusively from its associated feeder tank: Engine 1 from the left feeder, Engine 2 from the center feeder, and Engine 3 from the right feeder.

| Tank | Location | Capacity |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Left Wing Tank** | Left wing | 3,748 lb |
| **Center Tank** | Center fuselage | 2,749 lb |
| **Right Wing Tank** | Right wing | 3,748 lb |
| **Left Feeder Tank** | Rear fuselage | ≈ 1,400 lb |
| **Center Feeder Tank** | Rear fuselage | ≈ 2,460 lb |
| **Right Feeder Tank** | Rear fuselage | ≈ 1,400 lb |

Total usable fuel is **15,513.8 lb**.

## Fuel Flow

Fuel moves from storage to engines in three stages: **storage → regulation → engine feed**. Forward tanks are pressurized by low-pressure bleed air from Engines 1 and 2, creating a differential pressure that drives fuel toward the lower-pressure feeder tanks even with the transfer pumps off. Electrically driven transfer pumps <Badge color="yellow">1</Badge> in the forward tanks provide the primary transfer path, while the differential pressure system serves as an automatic backup.

Each feeder tank is automatically regulated to a nominal **609 lb** during flight. Above the regulation level, transfer is inhibited; below it, fuel is automatically drawn from the associated forward tank. From the feeder tank, an electric **booster pump** <Badge color="yellow">3</Badge> supplies positive-pressure fuel to its engine.

Within each wing, two **jet pumps** transfer fuel from the outboard sections to the inboard sections, ensuring the more aft (outboard) fuel is burned first to maintain a forward center of gravity throughout the flight.

## Abnormal Operations

The fuel system includes interconnect and crossfeed capabilities for redundancy. **Transfer interconnect valves** <Badge color="yellow">2</Badge> allow fuel from any forward tank to be routed to any feeder tank, which is useful for managing imbalance or routing fuel from a full tank when its associated forward tank is empty. **Crossfeed valves** <Badge color="yellow">4</Badge> allow one feeder tank to supply more than one engine, used following a booster pump or feeder tank failure.

<Warning>
Crossfeed operations should only be used during abnormal conditions. Monitor fuel quantities closely when using crossfeed, as a single feeder tank will then supply multiple engines.
</Warning>
