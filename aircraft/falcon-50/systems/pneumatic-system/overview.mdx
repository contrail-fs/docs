title: "Overview"
---

The Falcon 50 pneumatic system extracts compressed **bleed air** from the engines and, optionally, the APU to power a wide range of aircraft services. Bleed air feeds air conditioning, cabin pressurization, airframe and nacelle anti-icing, fuel tank pressurization, and hydraulic reservoir pressurization.

<Note>
Because the engines produce an abundance of compressed air, the pneumatic system simply taps a portion of it. There are no dedicated air compressors — all pneumatic supply comes from the engine bleed ports or the APU.
</Note>

## Bleed Ports

Each engine has **three bleed-air ports**: two Low-Pressure (LP) ports on the compressor case (at the 3 o'clock and 9 o'clock positions) and one High-Pressure (HP) port on top of the engine (12 o'clock), downstream of the centrifugal compressor. All ports feed the pneumatic system except the right-hand LP port of the No. 3 engine, which is capped off.

| Port | Location | Normal Use |
|------|----------|------------|
| **LP (×2 per engine)** | 3 and 9 o'clock | Air conditioning, pressurization, fuel and hydraulic reservoir pressurization |
| **HP (×1 per engine)** | 12 o'clock | Anti-icing, and air-conditioning make-up via the PRV |
| **Internal HP** | Within engine | That engine's own anti-icing |

Each bleed line carries **check valves** that establish an airflow priority and prevent air from flowing back into an engine that is shut down or running at low rpm.

## System Architecture

The pneumatic system is divided into a **main bleed-air system** and **secondary bleed-air systems**, both fed from a common distribution manifold.

| System | Source | Supplies |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Main bleed air** | All three engine LP/HP ports + APU (option) | Air conditioning, cabin / baggage / nose cone pressurization, airframe and S-duct anti-icing |
| **Secondary LP** | No. 1 and No. 2 engine LP ports | Fuel tank and hydraulic reservoir pressurization |
| **Secondary HP** | No. 1 and No. 2 engine HP ports + APU (option) | Jet pump for cabin pressurization outflow valve control |

## Normal Operation

In normal flight the **LP bleed system alone** supplies the aircraft. The HP system is normally **not used** — the HP valves stay closed and only open automatically when an anti-icing system is selected on. On the ground, the APU can supply the common manifold for air conditioning before the engines are providing sufficient air.

<Warning>
APU bleed air should **never** be used while the anti-icing systems are in use.
</Warning>
