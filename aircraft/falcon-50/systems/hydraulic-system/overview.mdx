title: "Overview"
---

The Falcon 50 hydraulic system delivers power to the flight controls, landing gear, brakes, and high-lift devices through **two fully independent systems** with no interconnection between them. Normal pressure is supplied by three engine-driven pumps at **3,000 psi (206 bar)**, with an electric stand-by pump providing emergency power and ground capability.

<Note>
The two hydraulic systems are completely independent — there is no possibility of interconnection between them. This separation ensures that a failure or leak in one system cannot affect the other, preserving control of the aircraft.
</Note>

## System Architecture

Hydraulic power is generated by three self-regulating, engine-driven pumps mounted on the accessory gear box of each engine. The left and center engine pumps supply **System 1**, while the right engine pump supplies **System 2**. An electric **stand-by pump** backs up System 2 in flight and can pressurize either system on the ground.

| System | Normal Sources | Surge Damping |
|--------|----------------|---------------|
| **System 1** | Engine 1 (left) and Engine 2 (center) pumps | Main accumulator + thrust reverser accumulator |
| **System 2** | Engine 3 (right) pump, backed up by the stand-by pump | Main accumulator + parking/emergency brake accumulator |

Each system supplies a distinct group of utilities, arranged so that essential functions remain available after the loss of either system.

| System 1 Utilities | System 2 Utilities |
|--------------------|--------------------|
| Rudder, elevator, and aileron servo-actuators (barrel 1) | Rudder, elevator, and aileron servo-actuators (barrel 2) |
| Elevator and aileron Arthur Q units | Flap motor |
| Inboard and outboard slat normal control | Airbrakes |
| Thrust reverser control | Outboard slat emergency control |
| Landing gear operation | Nose wheel steering |
| Normal braking | Parking / emergency braking |

## Pressure Generation

Each engine-driven pump delivers a regulated **3,000 psi (206 bar)** at zero flow, dropping to **2,850 psi (197 bar)** at maximum flow, with output proportional to engine N2. The fluid used is **MIL-H-5606** (NATO codes H515 or H520). System pressure is read on the right scale of each pressure/quantity indicator on the hydraulic panel, with the normal green range between **2,800 and 3,000 psi**.

On the ground, each system can be supplied either from an external hydraulic ground power unit or by the stand-by pump.

## Emergency and Ground Power

The electrically driven stand-by pump provides emergency hydraulic power to System 2 in flight and can be used to pressurize System 1 for ground testing in the absence of a ground power unit. It is controlled from the hydraulic panel by a three-position **STBY PUMP** lever (AUTO / ON / OFF).

<Warning>
Should a reservoir depressurization light (**TK.P1** or **TK.P2**) illuminate, monitor the fluid quantity and pressure indicators. If pressure begins to fluctuate, descend to an altitude below **20,000 ft** as soon as conditions permit.
</Warning>
