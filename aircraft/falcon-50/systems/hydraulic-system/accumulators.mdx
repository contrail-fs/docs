---
title: "Accumulators"
---

Each hydraulic system includes an **oleopneumatic accumulator** that stores hydraulic energy and acts as a surge damper, smoothing pressure fluctuations and supplying short bursts of demand without drawing directly on the pumps.

## Characteristics

Both main accumulators share the same characteristics:

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Maximum air volume** | 820 cu.cm (50 cu.in) |
| **Inflating pressure (on ground)** | 100 bar (1,450 psi) |
| **Hydraulic fluid volume** | 420 cu.cm (25.7 cu.in) at 207 bar (3,000 psi) |

Each accumulator is fitted with an **inflating valve** and a **pressure gage** graduated in psi.

## Additional Accumulators

Beyond the main accumulator in each system, two systems carry a dedicated accumulator for a specific function:

- **System 1** has an additional accumulator used by the **thrust reverser** system.
- **System 2** has an additional accumulator used by the **parking and emergency braking** system.

<Note>
The parking/emergency brake accumulator in System 2 retains stored pressure even after the system is depressurized, allowing the parking brake to remain set and providing a reserve of emergency braking applications.
</Note>

## Effect on Fluid Quantity

Because the accumulators store fluid when the system is pressurized, the reservoir level drops noticeably as each system comes up to pressure — about **13 mm in System 1** and **20 mm in System 2**. This is normal and should not be mistaken for a leak.
