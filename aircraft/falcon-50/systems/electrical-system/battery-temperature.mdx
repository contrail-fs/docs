---
title: "Battery Temperature"
---

A dedicated battery temperature monitoring system continuously measures the temperature of both batteries and alerts the pilots in the event of an overheat. Battery overheats can be caused by overcharging, internal cell failure, or excessive electrical loads, and require prompt pilot action.

## Indications

The battery temperature gauge is located on the main panel **below the copilot flight displays**. It provides a continuous temperature reading for each battery along with **WARM** and **HOT** annunciator lights that illuminate as the temperature crosses the published thresholds.

| Indication | Trigger Temperature | Annunciation |
|------------|--------------------|---------------|
| **WARM** light | 120 °F | Illuminates on the battery temperature gauge |
| **HOT** light | 150 °F | Illuminates on the battery temperature gauge |
| **HOT BAT** warning | 150 °F | Illuminates on the warning panel |

A WARM indication should be monitored closely and electrical loads reduced if possible. A HOT or HOT BAT indication requires immediate action in accordance with the published abnormal procedures, including isolating the affected battery from the bus.

<Warning>
A **HOT BAT** warning indicates a battery has reached **150 °F** and may be approaching thermal runaway. Take immediate action and isolate the affected battery without delay.
</Warning>

## Controls

The gauge carries two pushbuttons. The **TEST** button verifies the operation of the WARM and HOT warning lights and should be pressed during the cockpit power-up flow — both lights should illuminate while held and extinguish on release.

The **LESS 50** pushbutton allows the pilot to read off-scale values. When pressed, the gauge reading shifts down by 50 °F, so the actual temperature is obtained by adding 50 °F to the indicated value.

<Note>
Example: with LESS 50 pressed, a gauge reading of **130 °F** corresponds to an actual battery temperature of **180 °F**. This feature ensures the true temperature can always be read, even during severe overheat conditions.
</Note>
