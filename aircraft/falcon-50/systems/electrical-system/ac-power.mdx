---
title: "AC Power"
---

The Falcon 50 AC power system supplies **115-volt 400 Hz** alternating current to the avionics and other instruments. AC is produced by inverters that convert 28.5-volt DC into AC, which means the AC system is fully dependent on a healthy DC supply.

## Inverters

Three inverters are installed in the aircraft. The pilot inverter draws DC from the A bus and feeds the pilot's instruments and avionics, while the copilot inverter draws from the B bus and feeds the copilot's instruments and avionics. Because each primary inverter is fed from a different DC bus, an electrical fault on one side of the DC system affects only one AC bus.

| Inverter | Power Source | Function |
|----------|--------------|----------|
| **Pilot inverter** | A bus | Supplies AC power to the pilot's instruments and avionics |
| **Copilot inverter** | B bus | Supplies AC power to the copilot's instruments and avionics |
| **Standby inverter** | — | Backup unit, replaces the pilot or copilot inverter on failure |

A **standby inverter** is available to replace either primary inverter following a failure, preserving AC power to the affected pilot's flight instruments.

<Warning>
The standby inverter is **not simulated** in this product. AC power is always supplied by the pilot and copilot inverters in their normal configuration.
</Warning>

## Indications and Dependencies

A voltmeter on the overhead panel indicates AC bus voltage, and two pushbuttons next to it allow the pilot to select either the pilot or copilot AC bus for display. A reading outside the normal 115 VAC range during cockpit power-up indicates an inverter or upstream DC source issue that should be resolved before flight.

Because each inverter draws its DC input from a primary distribution bus, AC power availability mirrors the state of the DC system: loss of the A bus removes the pilot AC bus, and loss of the B bus removes the copilot AC bus. Restoring the upstream DC bus, for example by closing a bus tie, restores the corresponding AC bus.
