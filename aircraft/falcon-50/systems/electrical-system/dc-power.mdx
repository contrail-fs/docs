---
title: "DC Power"
---

The Falcon 50 DC system operates at **28.5 volts** and is the aircraft's primary electrical system. Power flows from any of three engine generators, the APU generator, two batteries, or external ground power into a dual main-bus structure with extensive cross-tie capability.

## Bus Architecture

DC power is distributed through a **Left Hand bus** and a **Right Hand bus**. Each main bus feeds a primary distribution bus and an auxiliary distribution bus, and three bus ties allow the pilot to isolate or connect busses for normal operations or fault isolation.

| Main Bus | Primary | Auxiliary | Normal Sources |
|----------|---------|-----------|----------------|
| **Left Hand Bus** | A | C | Generator 1, Generator 2, BAT1 |
| **Right Hand Bus** | B | D | Generator 3, BAT2 |

Generator 1 and Generator 2 both normally power the Left Hand bus, while Generator 3 alone powers the Right Hand bus. This arrangement preserves Right Hand bus power even after a complete failure of Engines 1 and 2.

<Tip>
Bus ties are the primary tool for managing electrical faults in flight. Closing the appropriate bus tie can restore power to a dead bus from a healthy source on the opposite side.
</Tip>

## Power Sources

Two **28.5-volt batteries** provide independent power to each side of the system: BAT1 normally feeds the Left Hand bus and BAT2 normally feeds the Right Hand bus. The batteries supply power before engine start, during APU start, and as emergency backup if all generators fail.

Three **engine-driven generators** provide the primary source of in-flight DC power:

| Generator | Driven By | Normal Bus |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| **Generator 1** | Engine 1 | Left Hand bus |
| **Generator 2** | Engine 2 | Left Hand bus |
| **Generator 3** | Engine 3 | Right Hand bus |

The **APU generator**, driven by the auxiliary power unit, can supply DC power on the ground or in flight within APU operating limits. It is commonly used for engine start and during ground operations to keep the batteries off-line.

## Controls and Indications

Battery and engine generator operation is controlled by five switches on the top of the overhead panel: **BAT1**, **BAT2**, **GEN 1**, **GEN 2**, and **GEN 3**. The APU generator is controlled by a dedicated pushbutton on the APU panel on the right console, with APU generator load indicated on a gauge on the same panel.

Two voltmeters on the overhead allow the pilot to monitor the voltage on each main DC bus, and pushbuttons next to them allow the amperage draw of each individual source (batteries, engine generators, and APU generator) to be displayed in turn. Monitoring source amperage is particularly important when operating with reduced generator capacity, since sustained operation above the rated output of any generator can cause overheating and eventual failure.

## Hot Battery Bus

A **hot battery bus** continuously supplies power to a small number of critical systems whenever the batteries are connected, regardless of the position of the master switches. Powered systems include:

- Emergency lighting
- Fire extinguishing
- Refueling system
- Other safety-critical items

<Note>
Because the hot battery bus is always energized when batteries are connected, the batteries can be slowly discharged even with the aircraft otherwise shut down if it is left unattended for long periods.
</Note>
