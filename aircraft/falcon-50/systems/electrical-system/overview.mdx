The Falcon 50 electrical system provides aircraft power through a **28.5-volt DC** primary system and a **115-volt 400 Hz AC** secondary system. DC power is generated by multiple independent sources, while AC power for avionics and instruments is produced by inverters.

<Note>
The system is designed around **dual-bus architecture** with extensive cross-tie capability. Either side of the electrical system can power the entire aircraft if the opposite side fails, providing significant redundancy for in-flight operations.
</Note>

## System Architecture

The electrical system follows a layered structure: **Sources → Main Busses → Distribution Busses**

### Power Sources

The aircraft can draw power from any of the following sources:

| Source | Type | Notes |
|--------|------|-------|
| **Battery 1 (BAT1)** | DC | Primary battery, normally feeds the Left Hand bus |
| **Battery 2 (BAT2)** | DC | Primary battery, normally feeds the Right Hand bus |
| **Generator 1** | DC | Driven by Engine 1 |
| **Generator 2** | DC | Driven by Engine 2 |
| **Generator 3** | DC | Driven by Engine 3 |
| **APU Generator** | DC | Driven by the APU |
| **Ground Power Unit (GPU)** | DC | 28-volt external power, connected behind the left wing |

### Main Bus Structure

DC power is distributed through two main busses, which in turn power four downstream distribution busses:

| Main Bus | Distribution Busses | Normal Power Sources |
|----------|---------------------|----------------------|
| **Left Hand Bus** | Primary A bus, Auxiliary C bus | Generator 1, Generator 2, BAT1 |
| **Right Hand Bus** | Primary B bus, Auxiliary D bus | Generator 3, BAT2 |

Three **bus ties** allow the pilot to isolate or connect busses as required for normal operations or fault isolation.

### AC Power Distribution

AC power is produced by **three inverters** that convert 28.5-volt DC into 115-volt 400 Hz AC for the avionics and other instruments:

- **Pilot inverter** — Powered from the A bus
- **Copilot inverter** — Powered from the B bus
- **Standby inverter** — Backup unit (not simulated)

## Controls and Indications

### Overhead Panel

Battery and engine generator operation is controlled by **five switches** located on the top of the overhead panel. A **power selector switch** on the overhead enables external (ground) power when connected.

Two voltmeters allow the pilot to view the voltage on each main DC bus, and pushbuttons allow viewing the amperage draw of each individual source. A separate AC voltmeter and two pushbuttons on the overhead allow the pilot to view the voltage of each AC bus.

### APU Panel

The APU generator is controlled by a **pushbutton on the APU panel** on the right console. APU generator load is indicated on a dedicated gauge on the same panel.

### Battery Temperature Indicator

A battery temperature gauge is located on the main panel below the copilot flight displays. It provides continuous temperature monitoring and warning indications for both batteries.

## Hot Battery Bus

A **hot battery bus** continuously supplies power to a small number of critical systems regardless of the position of the master switches. These include:

- Emergency lighting
- Fire extinguishing
- Refueling system
- Other safety-critical items

<Note>
The hot battery bus is energized at all times whenever a battery is connected to the aircraft. This ensures critical safety systems remain available even with the entire aircraft electrically shut down.
</Note>

## Redundancy and Fault Tolerance

The electrical system is designed for graceful degradation:

- **Bus ties** allow either main bus to be powered from the opposite side
- **Multiple generators** ensure power remains available after a single generator failure
- **Two batteries** provide independent emergency power to each side of the system
- **Standby inverter** can replace either the pilot or copilot inverter following a failure
- **Ground power** can fully power the aircraft on the ground without using the batteries

<Warning>
Batteries are automatically isolated from the electrical system whenever external (ground) power is in use. Verify the desired power source after disconnecting ground power.
</Warning>
