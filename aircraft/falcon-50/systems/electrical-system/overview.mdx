title: "Overview"
---

The Falcon 50 electrical system provides aircraft power through a **28.5-volt DC** primary system and a **115-volt 400 Hz AC** secondary system. DC power is generated by multiple independent sources, while AC power for avionics and instruments is produced by inverters that convert DC into AC.

<Note>
The system is designed around a **dual-bus architecture** with extensive cross-tie capability. Either side of the electrical system can power the entire aircraft if the opposite side fails, providing significant redundancy for in-flight operations.
</Note>

## System Architecture

The aircraft can draw DC power from any of two batteries (BAT1 and BAT2), three engine generators (one per engine), an APU generator, or a 28-volt ground power unit connected behind the left wing. These sources feed two main busses, each of which in turn powers a primary distribution bus and an auxiliary distribution bus.

| Main Bus | Primary | Auxiliary | Normal Sources |
|----------|---------|-----------|----------------|
| **Left Hand Bus** | A | C | Generator 1, Generator 2, BAT1 |
| **Right Hand Bus** | B | D | Generator 3, BAT2 |

AC power is produced by three inverters that convert 28.5-volt DC into 115-volt 400 Hz AC. The pilot inverter draws from the A bus and feeds the pilot's avionics, the copilot inverter draws from the B bus and feeds the copilot's avionics, and a standby inverter is available as a backup (not simulated). Three bus ties allow the pilot to isolate or connect main and distribution busses as required.

## Controls and Indications

Battery and engine generator operation is controlled by five switches on the top of the overhead panel. The same panel carries two DC voltmeters (one per main bus), pushbuttons for viewing the amperage draw of each individual source, an AC voltmeter with selector pushbuttons for the pilot and copilot AC busses, and a power selector switch that enables external ground power.

The APU generator is controlled by a dedicated pushbutton on the APU panel on the right console, with APU generator load shown on a gauge on the same panel. A battery temperature gauge below the copilot flight displays continuously monitors both batteries and provides WARM and HOT overheat indications.

## Redundancy

The electrical system is designed for graceful degradation. Bus ties allow either main bus to be powered from the opposite side, the two batteries provide independent emergency power to each side, the standby inverter can replace either primary inverter following a failure, and ground power can fully power the aircraft on the ground without using the batteries.

<Warning>
Batteries are automatically isolated from the electrical system whenever external (ground) power is in use. Verify the desired power source after disconnecting ground power.
</Warning>
