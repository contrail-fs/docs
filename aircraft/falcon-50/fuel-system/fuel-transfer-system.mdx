---
title: "Fuel Transfer System"
---

The fuel transfer system moves fuel from the forward tanks to the feeder tanks and manages fuel distribution within the wing tanks.

## Differential Pressure Transfer

Fuel flows from the forward tanks toward the feeder tanks due to **differential pressure created by tank pressurization**.

- This transfer path exists **independently of transfer pump operation**
- Fuel can flow through the transfer system even when transfer pumps are OFF or inoperative

<Tip>
This design ensures continued feeder tank replenishment without sole reliance on electrical pumps. Even if transfer pumps fail, fuel will continue to flow toward feeder tanks as long as pressurization is maintained.
</Tip>

## Transfer Pumps (XFR Pumps)

Each subsystem is equipped with an electrically driven **transfer pump**.

Transfer pumps:

- Provide the primary means of transferring fuel from forward tanks toward feeder tanks
- Supply excess fuel flow and pressure used by the wing jet pumps
- Permit fuel flow through the transfer system when not operating

<Info>
Transfer pumps are normally switched OFF once the associated forward tank is empty. They are located in the forward tanks and pump fuel toward their respective feeder tanks.
</Info>

## Wing Jet Pumps

Two **wing jet pumps** are installed, one in each wing. These venturi-type pumps transfer fuel from the **outboard wing section to the inboard wing section**, ensuring outboard fuel is burned first to support center-of-gravity management.

**Power source:**
- Left wing jet pump: Powered by excess fuel flow and pressure from **Transfer Pump No. 1**
- Right wing jet pump: Powered by excess fuel flow and pressure from **Transfer Pump No. 3**

**Operation:**
- Jet pumps operate whenever their associated transfer pump is operating
- Jet pumps do **not** feed the feeder tanks directly

**Failure mode:**
- If a transfer pump fails, fuel continues to move from the outboard to the inboard wing section by **gravity through flapper valves**

This design ensures outboard (more aft) fuel is burned first, maintaining a forward center of gravity throughout the flight.

## Transfer Interconnect Valves

Two **transfer interconnect valves** allow fuel from any forward tank to be routed to any feeder tank as required.

- Controlled from the overhead fuel panel
- Used for fuel management in abnormal or imbalance conditions
- Check valves prevent unintended lateral fuel transfer between wing tanks

<Tip>
Use interconnect valves to balance fuel distribution or route fuel from a full tank to a feeder tank when its associated forward tank is empty.
</Tip>
