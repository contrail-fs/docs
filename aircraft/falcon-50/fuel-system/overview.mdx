The Falcon 50 fuel system supplies all three engines through a **feeder-tank architecture** with automatic quantity regulation, multiple transfer paths, and built-in redundancy. Engines are never supplied directly from wing or center tanks.

The system is arranged as **three normally independent subsystems**: **left, center, and right**.

## System Architecture (At a Glance)

### Storage → Regulation → Engine Feed

1. **Forward Tanks (Storage)**
   - Left Wing Tank (3,748 lb)
   - Center Tank (2,749 lb)
   - Right Wing Tank (3,748 lb)
2. **Feeder Tanks (Regulated Supply)**
   - Left Feeder Tank (max ≈ 1,400 lb)
   - Center Feeder Tank (max ≈ 2,460 lb)
   - Right Feeder Tank (max ≈ 1,400 lb)
   - In-flight regulation level: **609 lb**
3. **Engine Feed**
   - Each engine is supplied exclusively from its associated feeder tank via a **booster pump**.

**Total usable fuel:** **15,513.8 lb**

## Fuel Transfer and Pressurization

### Differential Pressure Transfer

- Wing and center tanks are pressurized by **low-pressure bleed air from Engine 1 and Engine 2**.
- Fuel flows toward the feeder tanks due to **differential pressure**, independent of transfer pump operation.
- Wing tanks are maintained at higher pressure than feeder tanks to assist transfer and prevent cavitation.

### Transfer Pumps (XFR Pumps)

- Electrically driven **transfer pumps** provide the primary means of moving fuel from forward tanks toward feeder tanks.
- Fuel can flow through the transfer system even when transfer pumps are not operating.
- Transfer pumps are normally switched OFF once the associated forward tank is empty.

## Wing Fuel Management

### Wing Jet Pumps

- Two **wing jet pumps** transfer fuel from **outboard wing sections to inboard wing sections**.
- Operate on the **venturi principle**.
- Powered by excess fuel flow and pressure from:
  - Transfer Pump No. 1 (left wing)
  - Transfer Pump No. 3 (right wing)
- Jet pumps operate whenever the associated transfer pump is operating.
- In the event of transfer pump failure, fuel continues to move inboard by **gravity through flapper valves**.

This ensures outboard (more aft) fuel is burned first, maintaining a forward center of gravity.

## Feeder Tank Regulation

- Each feeder tank is automatically maintained at **609 lb** during flight.
- If feeder quantity exceeds regulation level, transfer from forward tanks is inhibited until the quantity decreases to regulation.
- If feeder quantity drops below regulation, fuel is transferred from the associated forward tanks.
- When remaining fuel is insufficient, feeder tank quantity decreases normally.

## Interconnect and Crossfeed Capability

### Transfer Interconnect Valves

- Allow fuel from any forward tank to be routed to any feeder tank.
- Used for fuel management in abnormal or imbalance conditions.
- Check valves prevent unintended lateral fuel transfer between wing tanks.

### Crossfeed Valves

- Allow one feeder tank to supply more than one engine.
- Normally closed.
- Used following booster pump or feeder tank failures.

## Cockpit Indications

- **Fuel Quantity Gauges**
  - **REAR:** feeder tank quantity only (normal position)
  - **TOT:** total fuel for the selected subsystem (forward tank + feeder tank)
- When a forward tank is empty, **609 lb** will continue to be indicated, representing feeder tank fuel.
