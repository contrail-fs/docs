The Falcon 50 forward fuel tanks provide **fuel storage only**. They serve as the primary fuel reservoirs and do not supply fuel directly to the engines.

<Warning>
Forward tanks never supply engines directly. All engine feed occurs exclusively through feeder tanks, which receive fuel from the forward tanks via the transfer system.
</Warning>

## Tank Arrangement and Capacities

The fuel system includes three forward tanks arranged in a left-center-right configuration:

| Tank | Location | Usable Fuel Capacity |
|------|----------|---------------------|
| **Left Wing Tank** | Left wing | **3,748 lb** |
| **Center Tank** | Center fuselage | **2,749 lb** |
| **Right Wing Tank** | Right wing | **3,748 lb** |

<Note>
**Total forward tank capacity:** 10,245 lb

Each wing tank is subdivided into **outboard** and **inboard** sections by internal structural baffles. This subdivision supports controlled fuel distribution within the wing tanks.
</Note>

## Wing Fuel Distribution

Fuel within each wing tank is managed to ensure that fuel from the **outboard (more aft) sections** is consumed first. This design supports a more forward center of gravity throughout the flight.

### Distribution Mechanism

This redistribution is achieved using:

- **Wing jet pumps** — Transfer fuel from outboard to inboard sections using venturi principles
- **Passive gravity features** — Flapper valves allow gravity-fed fuel movement when jet pumps are inoperative

<Info>
The wing jet pumps are powered by excess fuel flow from the transfer pumps. For detailed information on jet pump operation, see the [Fuel Transfer System](/aircraft/falcon-50/fuel-system/fuel-transfer-system) section.
</Info>

<Tip>
The outboard-first consumption pattern ensures that as fuel burns, the aircraft's center of gravity shifts forward, improving longitudinal stability and handling characteristics.
</Tip>

## Tank Pressurization

All forward tanks are pressurized by **low-pressure bleed air from Engine 1 and Engine 2**.

### Pressurization System

- **Automatic operation:** Pressurization occurs automatically when either engine is operating
- **Pressure differential:** Forward tanks are maintained at a **higher pressure than the feeder tanks**
- **Transfer mechanism:** This pressure differential enables fuel transfer toward the feeder tanks by **differential pressure**, independent of transfer pump operation

<Info>
The pressurization system provides a backup transfer mechanism. Even if transfer pumps fail, fuel will continue to flow from forward tanks to feeder tanks as long as engine bleed air pressurization is maintained.
</Info>

## Transfer System Integration

Forward tanks are connected to the fuel transfer system, which moves fuel to the feeder tanks:

- **Transfer pumps** provide the primary means of moving fuel from forward tanks to feeder tanks
- **Differential pressure** provides a secondary transfer path that operates independently of pump operation
- **Interconnect valves** allow fuel from any forward tank to be routed to any feeder tank when needed

For complete details on the transfer system operation, see the [Fuel Transfer System](/aircraft/falcon-50/fuel-system/fuel-transfer-system) documentation.
