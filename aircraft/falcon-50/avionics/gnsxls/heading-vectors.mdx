title: "Heading Vectors"
description: "Flying assigned headings and automatically intercepting a course using the HDG key on the GNS-XLS while staying coupled in NAV."
---

The Heading Vector function is accessed with the `HDG` key. It lets the FMS fly an assigned **heading** for you and, when you want, automatically roll onto and track a **course** — all while the autopilot stays coupled in **NAV (FMS)**. Use it for ATC radar vectors, joining a leg between two waypoints, or intercepting a final approach course.

<Note>
You do **not** switch between the autopilot's HDG and NAV modes to use this. You stay coupled in **NAV (FMS)** the whole time — the FMS flies the heading, then transitions to the course by itself. This mirrors the real aircraft.
</Note>

## Heading modes

The **HEADING MODE** line is cycled with the `BACK` key and offers three modes:

| Mode | Function |
|------|----------|
| **HDG SELECT** | Fly a heading and keep flying it (no automatic course join). Good for open-ended vectors. |
| **INTERCEPT** | Fly a heading until it intercepts a chosen course, then automatically join and track it. |
| **CANCEL** | Turn heading mode off and return to the flight plan. |

<Note>
A **?** after a mode word means it is not active yet. Press the `ENTER` key to activate it.
</Note>

## Flying a heading (ATC vectors)

1. Confirm the autopilot is coupled in **NAV**.
2. Press the `HDG` key. The cursor is positioned over the heading field.
3. Insert the assigned heading (e.g. `250`).

<Note>
Use an `R` or `L` prefix only for a turn of more than 180° (e.g. `L250`) to force the correct turn direction. Otherwise leave it off.
</Note>

4. Press the `ENTER` key. The cursor moves to the mode line, which displays **HDG SELECT?**.
5. Press the `ENTER` key again. The aircraft turns to and holds the heading, and **HDG SELECT** is displayed on the **NAV** page.

To change the heading later, press the `HDG` key, insert the new heading, and press the `ENTER` key. It takes effect immediately.

## Intercepting a course between two waypoints

1. Confirm the autopilot is coupled in **NAV**, flying (or about to fly) an assigned heading.
2. Press the `HDG` key, insert the heading, and press the `ENTER` key.
3. Press the `BACK` key to display **INTERCEPT?**, then press the `ENTER` key. The cursor moves to the **TO** field.
4. Press the `BACK` key to step through your flight plan to the waypoint you want to fly toward (or type its name), then press the `ENTER` key.
5. **DTK** shows the course. Leave it for the leg's own course, or insert a different course to intercept, then press the `ENTER` key.
6. An **OK? ENTER** prompt appears — press the `ENTER` key.

The aircraft flies the heading, and when it reaches the course it rolls on and tracks it, then continues the flight plan.

<Note>
If the geometry will not work, an intercept message is displayed. See [Intercept messages](#intercept-messages) below.
</Note>

## Intercepting a final approach course

The procedure is the same as intercepting a course, using the **FAF** as the target.

1. Confirm the autopilot is coupled in **NAV** on the ATC-assigned heading.
2. Press the `HDG` key, insert the heading, and press the `ENTER` key.
3. Press the `BACK` key to display **INTERCEPT?**, then press the `ENTER` key.
4. Press the `BACK` key until the **FAF** waypoint is shown in the **TO** field, then press the `ENTER` key.
5. In **DTK**, insert the published final approach course if it differs from the shown value, then press the `ENTER` key.
6. An **OK? ENTER** prompt appears — press the `ENTER` key.

The aircraft intercepts the final approach course and sequences toward the missed approach point.

<Tip>
Have the approach loaded in the flight plan first so the **FAF** is available to select.
</Tip>

## Canceling heading mode

Use either method:

- Press the `HDG` key, position the cursor over the mode line, press the `BACK` key to display **CANCEL?**, then press the `ENTER` key.
- Perform a [Direct To](/aircraft/falcon-50/avionics/gnsxls/direct-to) with the `D→` key — this cancels heading mode automatically.

## Intercept messages

| Message | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| *(no message)* | The course will be intercepted before the fix. |
| **INTERCEPT BEYOND FIX** | On this heading you would cross the course past the fix. Pick a heading that turns you toward the course sooner. |
| **NO COURSE INTERCEPT** | This heading takes you away from the course. Turn toward it. |
| **NO ARC INTERCEPT** | (DME arc targets) The heading will not reach the arc. |

## Known limitation

You can intercept a course to a **waypoint that is in your flight plan** (or an airport via Direct To). You currently **cannot** enter a random off-plan fix or an arbitrary course to a point that is not in the plan.

<Note>
Workaround: add the fix to the flight plan first, then intercept it.
</Note>

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I need to touch the autopilot HDG button?">
    No. Stay in **NAV**. The FMS flies the heading for you through the HDG page.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What's the difference between this and the autopilot's own HDG mode?">
    The autopilot HDG mode flies the heading bug and ignores the FMS. The HDG page keeps you coupled to the FMS so it can automatically join the course afterwards. For a clean automatic intercept, use the HDG page in **NAV**.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="R or L — when do I need it?">
    Only when the turn is more than 180° from your current heading, to force the correct turn direction. Otherwise leave it off.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Nothing happened when I entered a heading.">
    Make sure the autopilot is coupled in **NAV** and that you pressed the `ENTER` key on the mode line (a **?** means the mode is not active yet).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
