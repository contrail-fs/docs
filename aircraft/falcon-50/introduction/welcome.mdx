The Dassault Falcon 50 is a three-engine, long-range business jet that first flew in 1976 and entered service in 1979. It was Dassault's answer to the growing demand for an aircraft that could cross continents and oceans non-stop, and it became the first purpose-built trijet in the business aviation market.

The Falcon 50's DNA traces directly back to Dassault's roots as a builder of military combat aircraft. The company's experience with the Mirage family of fighters carried over into the design of an all-new supercritical wing, developed with input from that high-performance heritage. That wing, paired with three fuel-efficient turbofans, gave the Falcon 50 the transcontinental range, high cruise speed, and clean handling that few aircraft of its era could match — and a reputation for reliability and reach that defined the type.

Over its production run, the Falcon 50 evolved into the improved Falcon 50EX, which introduced more powerful and efficient engines along with a modernized cockpit. Together, the two variants sold in the hundreds and built a loyal following among operators who valued the aircraft's three-engine safety margin on long overwater legs.

## Operators, past and present

From the start, the Falcon 50 attracted a mix of civil and government customers. Several air forces and government fleets adopted it for VIP and head-of-state transport, where its range and three-engine redundancy were especially valued. A maritime patrol variant, the Falcon 50M, served in surveillance and search-and-rescue roles.

Today the type is most commonly seen as a private and charter business jet, with the largest active fleet operating in the United States. Many of these aircraft have passed through multiple owners and operators over the decades, and a healthy market for upgrades and refurbishment keeps them flying. It remains a popular choice for operators who want intercontinental capability in a comparatively compact airframe.

## Our aircraft

This is our first aircraft release, and it has been roughly two and a half years in the making. We're genuinely proud of the state it ships in, and just as proud of the airplane we chose to bring to Microsoft Flight Simulator.

The Falcon 50 is, in our eyes, unmatched in design and aesthetics — a graceful trijet that still turns heads on any ramp. It's also the first Falcon to arrive in Microsoft Flight Simulator, which made it an easy aircraft to fall in love with as a project.

What really drew us in is the cockpit. The Falcon 50 sits at a fascinating crossroads between classic and modern: a blend of traditional steam gauges alongside more contemporary avionics. We modelled the commonly seen EFIS upgrade — a digital Collins 85C EFIS paired with a Garmin MX20 MFD and a GNS-XLS Flight Management Computer — which gives the panel its distinctive mixed character.

Most current releases lean toward full modern Garmin glass cockpits. We wanted to do something different. To match the story of an aircraft that has been flying — and quietly upgraded — for decades, we've given the cockpit a used, lived-in look rather than the showroom finish of an aircraft fresh off the assembly line. Given the average age of these airframes today, that feels like the honest way to represent them, and we think it adds a real sense of character to every flight.
